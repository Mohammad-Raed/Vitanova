Part 1

Explanation

Course at a glance

  • Introduction to Medical Immunology, third-year medical students, 2026–2027.
  • 2 credit hours; 26 contact hours made up of 19 lectures.
  • 13 weeks, two sessions per week.
  • Lecturer: Prof. Dr. Hazem Aqel, Medical Microbiology and Immunology. Inquiries go to the coordinator by e-mail (address on the slides).

Why study immunology?

The slides list six areas where immunity matters clinically:

  • Vaccines – from smallpox to mRNA and RSV vaccines.
  • Allergy – common, and anaphylaxis is a medical emergency.
  • Autoimmunity – about 1 in 10 people (lupus, RA, type 1 diabetes, MS).
  • Transplantation – kidney, liver, stem cells, and now gene-edited pig organs.
  • Immunotherapy – checkpoint inhibitors, CAR-T cells, bispecific antibodies.
  • Laboratory tests – serology, flow cytometry, PCR and HLA typing guide daily decisions.
Flag: the allergy line on the slide is partly a graphic; a “about 1 in 3” figure appears only in the picture, not in text, so it is not repeated here as a fact. The lecturer also added spoken comments (in the note-taker’s Arabic notes) that some people are allergic to certain foods or drugs and that immune strength affects the severity and duration of disease.

The big idea: the order of the course

Normal immunity→How we measure it→Disease→Therapy

The course is built so that each lecture depends on the previous ones, which is why the slides tell you to study in order.

Objectives ladder

Know→Understand→Interpret→Apply

Each lecture is built around objectives, and the slide style is: recognise → mechanism → confirm → treat. Lectures are about 18–20 slides each, so you know what is expected before you start, and the summary tables serve as your revision sheet.

Attendance rules

  • Attendance is recorded in every session, and university rules apply.
  • 10% absence → first written warning; 15% → second written warning; 25% or more → course forfeited, with no entry to the final exam.
  • Excuses are discussed with the instructor and need written proof.
  • Phones off and laptops closed during class.
Flag: the note-taker says these rules are reportedly not being enforced at the moment and could be applied under the new dean. That is a comment, not slide content, so treat the slide rules as the official ones.

Study tips on the last slides

  • Read the objectives and look at the figures; ask questions and work through each case.
  • Redraw one key figure from memory; use the summary tables and link mechanisms to diseases.

References listed

Main: Abbas, Lichtman and Pillai, Basic Immunology (7th ed., 2024); Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Immunology; the lecturer’s handouts and slides. Additional: Janeway’s Immunobiology, Stevens and Miller Clinical Immunology and Serology, Roitt’s Essential Immunology.
