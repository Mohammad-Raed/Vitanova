  • Lecturer: Prof. Dr. Hazem Aqel, Medical Microbiology and Immunology. Inquiries go to the coordinator by e-mail (address on the slides).

Why study immunology?

The slides list six areas where immunity matters clinically:

  • Vaccines – from smallpox to messenger RNA (mRNA) and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) vaccines.
  • Allergy – common, and anaphylaxis is a medical emergency.
  • Autoimmunity – about 1 in 10 people (lupus, RA (rheumatoid arthritis), type 1 diabetes, MS (multiple sclerosis)).
  • Transplantation – kidney, liver, stem cells, and now gene-edited pig organs.
  • Immunotherapy – checkpoint inhibitors, CAR-T cells, bispecific antibodies.
  • Laboratory tests – serology, flow cytometry, PCR (polymerase chain reaction) and HLA (human leukocyte antigen) typing guide daily decisions.

Food and drug allergies are common. Immune status can influence the severity and duration of allergic disease.

The big idea: the order of the course

Normal immunity→How we measure it→Disease→Therapy

Course concepts build cumulatively, so the slides recommend studying the lectures in sequence.
