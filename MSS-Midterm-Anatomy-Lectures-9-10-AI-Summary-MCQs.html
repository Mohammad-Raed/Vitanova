Lecture 9

The Arm & Cubital Fossa

Compartments

Deep fascia forms the medial and lateral intermuscular septa, attached to the supracondylar ridges of the humerus. They divide the arm into anterior (flexors) and posterior (extensors) compartments, each with its own muscles, nerves and arteries.

Clinical link: Musculocutaneous injury weakens but does not abolish elbow flexion, because brachialis is also supplied by the radial nerve. Biceps is most effective at supinating a flexed elbow.

Nerves of the arm

Musculocutaneous
  • Lateral cord, C5–7
  • Pierces coracobrachialis, runs between biceps and brachialis
  • Pierces deep fascia above elbow as lateral cutaneous nerve of forearm
Median
  • Medial + lateral cords
  • Lateral to brachial artery, crosses anterior to it mid-arm, then medial
  • No branches in the arm (only a vasomotor twig)
Ulnar
  • Medial cord
  • Medial to artery until mid-arm; pierces medial septum at coracobrachialis insertion with superior ulnar collateral artery
  • Passes behind the medial epicondyle; no branches in the arm
Radial
  • Posterior cord
  • Spiral groove between triceps heads with profunda brachii
  • Pierces lateral septum, enters cubital fossa between brachialis and brachioradialis
  • Branches: triceps, anconeus, lower lateral cutaneous of arm, posterior cutaneous of forearm; in front: brachialis, brachioradialis, ECRL, elbow articular

Brachial artery

Axillary artery→Begins at lower border of teres major→Ends opposite radial neck→Radial + ulnar arteries
  • Branches: muscular, nutrient (humerus), profunda brachii (near origin, with radial nerve), superior ulnar collateral (mid-arm, with ulnar nerve), inferior ulnar collateral (near end)
  • Pulse: mid-arm in the cleft between biceps and triceps (BP cuff site); in the cubital fossa medial to the biceps tendon

Cubital fossa

Boundaries
  • Lateral: brachioradialis
  • Medial: pronator teres
  • Base: line between the humeral epicondyles
  • Floor: supinator (lateral) + brachialis (medial)
  • Roof: skin, superficial fascia, reinforced by bicipital aponeurosis
Contents, medial to lateral
Median nerve→Brachial artery bifurcation→Biceps tendon→Radial nerve (deep branch)
Clinical link: The median cubital vein (cephalic to basilic, diagonal across the roof) is the usual site for venipuncture and cardiac catheters. The bicipital aponeurosis separates it from the brachial artery and median nerve. Tourniquet goes on mid-arm and is removed before needle withdrawal. The supratrochlear node drains the 3rd–5th fingers, medial hand and medial forearm to the lateral axillary nodes.

Lecture 10

Axillary Vessels & Brachial Plexus

Contents of the axilla

Axillary artery + branchesAxillary veinLymph nodesBrachial plexus

Axillary artery

  • Begins at lateral border of 1st rib (continues subclavian); ends at lower border of teres major (becomes brachial)
  • Enclosed with plexus cords in the axillary sheath
  • Pectoralis minor crosses in front and divides it into 3 parts: 1 · 2 · 3 branches (6 total). Mnemonic: Some Students Like To Study Anatomy
PartExtentBranchesRelations
1stLateral border of 1st rib to upper border of pec minorSuperior (highest) thoracicLateral: three cords; medial: axillary vein
2ndBehind pec minorThoracoacromial, lateral thoracicAnterior: pec minor, pec major, skin. Lateral cord laterally, posterior cord posteriorly (with subscapularis), medial cord + axillary vein medially
3rdLower border of pec minor to lower border of teres majorSubscapular, posterior and anterior circumflex humeral (wind round surgical neck)Continues as brachial artery

Axillary vein

  • Formed at lower border of teres major by venae comitantes of brachial artery + basilic vein
  • Runs on the medial side of the artery, ends at lateral border of 1st rib as the subclavian vein
  • Tributaries match the artery’s branches, plus the cephalic vein

Brachial plexus

Formed in the posterior triangle of the neck by anterior rami of C5–C8 and T1. Trunks pass between anterior and middle scalenes, behind the subclavian artery, over the 1st rib and lung apex.

5 Roots→3 Trunks (S, M, I)→6 Divisions (3 ant, 3 post)→3 Cords (L, P, M)→5 Terminal nerves
  • Lateral cord: superior + middle trunk anterior divisions
  • Posterior cord: posterior divisions of all three trunks
  • Medial cord: anterior division of inferior trunk alone
  • Cords are named by position relative to the 2nd part of the axillary artery

  • Axillary C5, 6

Function pattern
  • Anterior divisions (musculocutaneous, median, ulnar): flexors
  • Posterior divisions (axillary, radial): extensors
  • The “M” formed by the musculocutaneous, median and ulnar nerves helps identify them

Axillary lymph nodes (read only)
