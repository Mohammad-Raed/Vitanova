Lecture 3

Bones, curves & neural content

Regions and curves

Regions

C1–C7T1–T12L1–L5Sacrum S1–S5Coccyx ~4
  • Length ~72–75 cm; ¼ is discs
  • Sacrum and coccyx fuse by about age 30
  • Roles: protect cord, support head, attach ribs/pelvis/muscles, posture

Curves

  • Primary (concave anteriorly): thoracic and sacral, formed in the fetus
  • Secondary (convex anteriorly): cervical (head up, ~4 months) and lumbar (sitting/walking, ~1 year)
  • Functions: strength, balance, shock absorption

Abnormal curves

  • Kyphosis: excessive concavity (thoracic)
  • Lordosis: excessive convexity (lumbar)
  • Scoliosis: lateral deviation; the column should be straight in the anterior/posterior view
Lab link: on the articulated spine trace the four curves from the side, then check the back view for lateral deviation.

Intervertebral disc (IVD)

  • Annulus fibrosus: fibrocartilage, concentric lamellae, collagen bundles run obliquely in alternating directions (strength)
  • Nucleus pulposus: gelatinous centre; absorbs shock and gives flexibility
  • Thickest in cervical and lumbar regions (most movement); lumbar discs are thicker anteriorly (wedge shape) to suit the curve
  • Nucleus lies posteriorly and the posterior annulus is weaker, so herniation is posterolateral, mostly L4–L5 or L5–S1, compressing the nerve

Typical vertebra

Parts

  • Body: anterior, weight bearing
  • Arch: two pedicles (cylindrical) + two laminae (flat)
  • 7 processes: 1 spinous, 2 transverse (muscle attachment), 4 articular (2 superior, 2 inferior)

Foramina

  • Vertebral foramina stacked = spinal canal
  • Intervertebral foramen = superior + inferior vertebral notches of adjacent pedicles; transmits spinal nerve and vessels

Atypical cervical vertebrae

C1 Atlas

  • Ring: no body, no spinous process
  • Superior facets take the occipital condyles; widest cervical vertebra
  • Nodding “yes” (atlanto-occipital)

C2 Axis

  • Dens (odontoid) rises from its body
  • Has body, spinous process and transverse foramen
  • Rotation “no” (atlanto-axial pivot)

Sacrum & coccyx

  • Sacral promontory (anterosuperior margin); ala = fused transverse processes; median crest = fused spinous processes
  • Sacral hiatus where laminae of S4–S5 do not meet; sacral and coccygeal cornua flank it
  • Auricular surface and tuberosity form the sacroiliac joint with the hip bone
  • Anterior and posterior sacral foramina carry ventral and dorsal rami of S1–S4
Lab link: find the hiatus and cornua on the dry sacrum; this is the landmark for caudal epidural anaesthesia (Lecture 4).

Spinal cord, nerves & coverings

Cord

  • Ends at L2 in adults, L4 in newborns (column outgrows cord; growth of cord stops ~age 5)
  • Cervical enlargement = upper limb; lumbar enlargement = lower limb
  • Conus medullaris → filum terminale (pia extension, anchors to coccyx); cauda equina = lower roots

Roots

  • Dorsal root: sensory; ganglion holds sensory cell bodies (outside CNS)
  • Ventral root: motor; cell bodies in the anterior horn
  • Spinal nerve = mixed, then splits into dorsal and ventral rami

Back innervation

  • Posterior rami: skin and intrinsic back muscles
  • IVD and dura: recurrent meningeal branches (afferent + sympathetic)
  • Zygapophysial joints: articular branches of posterior rami
Lab link: in the opened canal identify cauda equina, filum terminale and the layers; remember that the cord ends L2 but the dural sac reaches S2.

Joints, ligaments & muscles

Joint types

Between bodies

  • Symphysis (cartilaginous): hyaline plates on bodies + IVD
  • Supply: meningeal branches

Between arches

  • Zygapophysial (facet): plane synovial between superior and inferior articular processes
  • Supply: articular branches of posterior rami

Lumbar region details

  • IVF narrows L1→L5 while nerves enlarge, so nerve encroachment is commoner low down
  • Facet orientation: T12–L1 sagittal; L5–S1 coronal (superior facets face medially, rotating to posterior)

Lumbar puncture vs epidural

Lumbar puncture

Withdraws CSF at L3–L4 or L4–L5, into the lumbar cistern (L2–S2), safely below the cord.

Skin→Supra/interspinous→Lig. flavum→Epidural→Dura→Arachnoid

Epidural anaesthesia

Same approach, but stops in the epidural space (a “give” after the ligamentum flavum). Dura is not pierced. Can also be given through the sacral hiatus (caudal).

Lab link: on the spine model palpate the L4 spinous level (iliac crest line) and name each layer a needle crosses.

High-yield summary

Numbers

  • 7 / 12 / 5 / 5 / 4
  • 31 spinal nerve pairs (8 cervical)
  • Cord ends L2 (newborn L4)
  • Dura and arachnoid end S2
  • LP at L3–L4 or L4–L5

Landmarks

  • Atlas: no body; axis: dens
  • C7: long spinous process
  • Costal facets: thoracic only
  • Sacral hiatus: S5 + Co1 exit, caudal epidural

Clinical

  • Herniation posterolateral L4–L5, L5–S1
  • Kyphosis ↑ concave, lordosis ↑ convex
  • LP pierces dura; epidural does not

Nerve supply

  • Intrinsic back muscles, skin, facet joints: posterior rami
  • IVD, dura: meningeal branches
  • Extrinsic superficial: anterior rami
