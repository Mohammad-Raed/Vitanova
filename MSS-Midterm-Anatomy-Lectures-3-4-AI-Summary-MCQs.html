Lecture 3

Bones, curves & neural content

Regions and curves

Regions

Level labels: C = cervical, T = thoracic, L = lumbar, S = sacral, and Co = coccygeal. A number identifies the vertebra or nerve within that region; the context tells you which.

C1–C7T1–T12L1–L5Sacrum S1–S5Coccyx ~4
  • Adult length varies with body size; intervertebral discs contribute roughly one quarter of the movable column’s length
  • The sacral vertebrae normally fuse during early adulthood; coccygeal number and fusion vary between individuals
  • Roles: protect cord, support head, attach ribs/pelvis/muscles, posture

Curves

  • Primary curves: thoracic and sacral, convex posteriorly (concave anteriorly); present during fetal development
  • Secondary curves: cervical and lumbar, convex anteriorly. They develop as head control and upright posture are acquired during infancy and childhood
  • Functions: strength, balance, shock absorption

Abnormal curves

  • Hyperkyphosis: excessive posterior convexity, usually of the thoracic region. “Kyphosis” also describes the normal thoracic curvature
  • Hyperlordosis: excessive anterior convexity, usually of the lumbar region. “Lordosis” also describes normal cervical and lumbar curvature
  • Scoliosis: a lateral spinal curve, usually with vertebral rotation; assess in the coronal view rather than confusing it with the normal side-view curves
Lab link: on the articulated spine trace the four curves from the side, then check the back view for lateral deviation.

Intervertebral disc (IVD)

  • Annulus fibrosus: fibrocartilage, concentric lamellae, collagen bundles run obliquely in alternating directions (strength)
  • Nucleus pulposus: gelatinous centre; absorbs shock and gives flexibility
  • Lumbar discs are thickest in absolute size. Cervical and lumbar discs permit substantial movement relative to vertebral-body height; anterior–posterior differences in disc height help shape the normal curves
  • Disc herniation is commonly posterolateral, particularly at L4–L5 or L5–S1, where it may compress a nerve root. A typical posterolateral L4–L5 herniation affects the traversing L5 root, and L5–S1 affects S1; foraminal herniations can affect the exiting root instead

Typical vertebra

Parts

  • Body: anterior, weight bearing
  • Arch: two pedicles (cylindrical) + two laminae (flat)
  • 7 processes: 1 spinous, 2 transverse (muscle attachment), 4 articular (2 superior, 2 inferior)

Foramina

  • Vertebral foramina stacked = spinal canal
  • Intervertebral foramen (IVF) = the opening between adjacent vertebrae, bounded partly by their pedicle notches; transmits the spinal nerve and accompanying vessels

Atypical cervical vertebrae

C1 Atlas

  • Ring: no body, no spinous process
  • Superior facets take the occipital condyles; widest cervical vertebra
  • Nodding “yes” (atlanto-occipital)

C2 Axis

  • Dens (odontoid) rises from its body
  • Has body, spinous process and transverse foramen
  • Rotation “no” (atlanto-axial pivot)

Sacrum & coccyx

  • Sacral promontory = the projecting anterior margin of the S1 body; alae = lateral wings of the upper sacrum; median sacral crest = fused spinous processes
  • Sacral hiatus results from non-fusion of the lowest sacral laminae, usually S5 and sometimes S4. Sacral cornua flank the opening; coccygeal cornua lie below and articulate with them
  • The auricular surface articulates with the ilium at the sacroiliac joint; the adjacent sacral tuberosity provides ligament attachments
  • Anterior and posterior sacral foramina carry ventral and dorsal rami of S1–S4
Lab link: find the hiatus and cornua on the dry sacrum; this is the landmark for caudal epidural anaesthesia (Lecture 4).

Spinal cord, nerves & coverings

Cord

  • The conus medullaris usually ends around the L1–L2 vertebral level in adults and around L2–L3 in newborns, with individual variation. Vertebral growth exceeds cord elongation, so lower nerve roots descend to reach their exit levels
  • Cervical enlargement = upper limb; lumbar enlargement = lower limb
  • Conus medullaris = the tapered end of the cord. The filum terminale continues inferiorly from it and contributes to fixation; cauda equina = descending lumbar, sacral and coccygeal nerve roots below the conus

Roots

  • Dorsal root: sensory; the dorsal root ganglion contains primary sensory neuron cell bodies outside the central nervous system (CNS)
  • Ventral root: carries motor fibres. Somatic motor neuron cell bodies lie in the anterior horn; autonomic preganglionic fibres arise at the relevant autonomic spinal levels
  • Spinal nerve = mixed, then splits into dorsal and ventral rami

31 pairs in total: 8 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral and 1 coccygeal (Co1).

Coverings, from the wall of the vertebral canal inward: the epidural space contains fat and a venous plexus; the subdural space is normally only potential.

Vertebral canal wall→Epidural space→Dura mater→Potential subdural space→Arachnoid mater→Subarachnoid space: cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)→Pia mater on the cord

The dural and arachnoid sacs usually extend to S2. Denticulate ligaments are lateral extensions of pia mater that help stabilise the cord.

Back innervation

  • Posterior rami: skin and intrinsic back muscles
  • IVD and dura: recurrent meningeal branches (afferent + sympathetic)
  • Zygapophysial joints: articular branches of posterior rami
Lab link: in the opened canal identify cauda equina, filum terminale and the layers; distinguish the adult conus near L1–L2 from the dural sac, which usually reaches S2.
Keep the levels separate: vertebral levels, spinal-cord segments and spinal-nerve exit levels are different labels. The adult conus is usually near L1–L2; the dural sac continues to about S2. These are typical ranges, not exact landmarks in every individual.

Joints, ligaments & muscles

Joint types

Between bodies

  • Symphysis (cartilaginous): hyaline plates on bodies + IVD
  • Supply: meningeal branches

Between arches

  • Zygapophysial (facet): plane synovial between superior and inferior articular processes
  • Supply: articular branches of posterior rami

Lumbar region details

  • Lower lumbar nerve roots are vulnerable where disc protrusion or degenerative changes narrow the lateral recess or IVF; do not infer compression from vertebral level alone
  • Lumbar facet joints are generally closer to the sagittal plane at upper levels and become more coronally oriented toward the lumbosacral junction. Orientation varies; the lower facets help resist forward translation

Lumbar puncture vs epidural

Lumbar puncture

Samples CSF from the lumbar subarachnoid space, usually through L3–L4 or L4–L5 in adults. These interspaces lie below the usual adult conus; the lumbar cistern extends from the conus to about S2 and contains the cauda equina.

Skin→Subcutaneous tissue→Supraspinous and interspinous ligaments→Ligamentum flavum→Epidural space→Dura mater→Arachnoid mater→Subarachnoid space (CSF)

Epidural anaesthesia

A midline lumbar approach crosses the posterior ligaments but stops in the epidural space (a “give” after the ligamentum flavum). Dura is not pierced. Can also be given through the sacral hiatus (caudal).

Lab link: the line joining the highest iliac crests is a useful approximate landmark near L4 or the L4–L5 interspace, not an exact level in every person. On the model, distinguish the target space for LP from the epidural target.

High-yield summary

Numbers

  • Vertebrae: 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 fused sacral, and usually 4 coccygeal
  • 31 spinal nerve pairs (8 cervical)
  • Conus usually near L1–L2 in adults and L2–L3 in newborns; levels vary
  • Dura and arachnoid end S2
  • LP at L3–L4 or L4–L5

Landmarks

  • Atlas: no body; axis: dens
  • C7: long spinous process
  • Costal facets: thoracic only
  • Sacral hiatus: S5 + Co1 exit, caudal epidural

Clinical

  • Herniation posterolateral L4–L5, L5–S1
  • Thoracic kyphosis is posteriorly convex; lumbar lordosis is anteriorly convex. Use “hyper-” for an excessive curve
  • LP pierces dura; epidural does not

Nerve supply

  • Intrinsic back muscles, skin, facet joints: posterior rami
  • IVD, dura: meningeal branches
  • Superficial extrinsic muscles: anterior-ramus-derived nerves, except trapezius motor supply via CN XI
