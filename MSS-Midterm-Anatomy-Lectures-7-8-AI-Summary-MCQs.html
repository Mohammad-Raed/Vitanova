Elbow joint

Articulation & capsule

  • Trochlea + capitulum of humerus with trochlear notch of ulna and head of radius; hyaline cartilage.
  • Capsule: above to coronoid, radial & olecranon fossae; below to coronoid margin, olecranon and the anular ligament.
Handwritten: the anular ligament receives 3 structures: lower part of capsule, posterior part of capsule, radial collateral ligament.

Ligaments IMP

  • Capsule thickens medially & laterally into collateral ligaments (support flexion/extension, the only movement).
  • Ulnar collateral: triangular band; anterior, posterior, inferior parts.
  • Radial collateral: low on lateral epicondyle to anular ligament.

Synovial membrane & bursae

  • Lines capsule, covers fat pads in coronoid, radial, olecranon fossae; continuous with proximal radioulnar joint.
  • Intratendinous: inside triceps tendon.
  • Subtendinous: olecranon ↔ triceps tendon (reduces friction).
  • Subcutaneous (olecranon): olecranon ↔ skin; olecranon bursitis.

Movement & supply

  • Carrying angle opens laterally: ≈170° male, 167° female; disappears in full flexion.
  • Nerves: median, ulnar, musculocutaneous, radial (she won’t ask). Blood: anastomosis around elbow.

Relations IMP Exam question for sure

Anterior: brachialis, biceps tendon, median nerve, brachial arteryPosterior: triceps, small bursaMedial: ulnar nerve behind medial epicondyle, crosses medial ligamentLateral: common extensor tendon & supinator
Handwritten (stability): stable because of the wrench-shaped olecranon + pulley-shaped trochlea and strong collaterals. More ligaments = more stability, but bone shape matters too, not only ligaments.
Clinical link: dislocation is most often posterior (fall on outstretched hand), common in children (stabilising bone parts undeveloped). Handwritten: radius & ulna are the bones that displace. Arthrocentesis: posteriorly, either side of the olecranon, where effusion bulges.
Lab link: palpate the ulnar nerve behind the medial epicondyle (“funny bone”) and the olecranon with its subcutaneous bursa.

Radioulnar joints

Proximal

  • Radial head circumference + anular ligament + radial notch of ulna.
  • Anular ligament attaches to anterior/posterior margins of the radial notch, forms a collar, not attached to the radius (just surrounds it).
  • Capsule continuous with elbow.
  • Relations: supinator anteriorly; supinator + common extensor tendon posteriorly.

Distal

  • Head of ulna + ulnar notch of radius; capsule deficient superiorly; weak anterior/posterior ligaments.
  • Articular disc IMP: triangular fibrocartilage; apex to base of ulnar styloid, base to lower border of ulnar notch. Shuts DRUJ off from wrist, strongly unites radius to ulna (extra stability).
  • Nerve: anterior interosseous + deep branch of radial.
  • Relations: FDP tendons anteriorly; extensor digiti minimi posteriorly.
Movements: pronation = pronator teres + pronator quadratus. Supination = biceps + supinator; supination is stronger (biceps). Handwritten: supination & pronation are controlled by the proximal radioulnar joint, not the elbow; the radial head spins.

Wrist and hand

Wrist (radiocarpal)

  • Radius + articular disc above; scaphoid, lunate, triquetral below.
  • Medial ligament: ulnar styloid → triquetral. Lateral: radial styloid → scaphoid.
  • Cavity does not communicate with DRUJ or intercarpal joints (handwritten: “like 2 separate joints”).
  • Nerve IMP: anterior interosseous (median), posterior interosseous (radial), dorsal & deep branches of ulnar. Blood: carpal arches.
  • No rotation (ellipsoid); compensated by pronation/supination. Abduction < adduction because radial styloid is longer.

Intercarpal / midcarpal

  • Intercarpal: between individual bones; midcarpal: between the two rows (handwritten: this explanation not required).
  • Strong anterior, posterior, interosseous ligaments; small gliding.
  • CMC & intermetacarpal joints (slide 24) were deleted by the doctor.

Thumb CMC IMP

  • Trapezium + saddle-shaped base of 1st metacarpal; own joint cavity.
  • Flexion: flexor pollicis brevis + opponens. Extension: EPL + EPB. Abduction: APL + APB. Adduction: adductor pollicis.
  • Opposition (“grabbing”): opponens pollicis.

MCP & IP IMP

  • MCP: condyloid. Palmar ligament (fibrocartilage, firm to phalanx), deep transverse metacarpal ligament unites palmar ligaments of digits 2–5, collaterals each side.
  • IP: hinge; palmar & collateral ligaments, extensor tendon posteriorly. Supplied by adjacent nerves/vessels.
Lab link: in the dissection, trace extensor digiti minimi (posterior) and FDP (anterior) at the distal radioulnar joint, and palpate both styloids: radial is longer and more distal.

Handwritten connections

  • Bicipital groove = intertubercular sulcus: lateral lip pec major, floor lat dorsi, medial lip teres major.
  • Trapezius insertion (inner aspect) = deltoid origin (outer aspect).
  • Levator scapulae shares insertion & nerve with rhomboids.
  • Deltoid shares axillary nerve with teres minor; supraspinatus shares suprascapular with infraspinatus.

Winged scapula Clinical

  • Serratus anterior paralysis from long thoracic nerve injury.
  • Medial border moves laterally & posteriorly; worse when pushing against a wall.
  • Cannot abduct above horizontal (glenoid not rotated upward).
Lab link: ask a partner to push a wall and inspect the scapula.

Rotator cuff (SITS)

  • Tendons of subscapularis, supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, fused to capsule.
  • Stabilises shoulder by holding the humeral head in the glenoid.
  • Lies anterior, superior, posterior; deficient inferiorly (weak point). Teres major is not part.

Spaces IMP

  • Quadrangular: teres minor above, teres major below, long head triceps medial, surgical neck lateral. Contains axillary nerve + posterior circumflex humeral vessels.
  • Triangular: long head triceps (medial border), teres major, teres minor. Contains circumflex scapular vessels. (Slide lists superior/inferior reversed; anatomically teres minor is superior, major inferior.)

The axilla

Shape & openings

  • Pyramid between upper arm and chest; passage from neck root to upper limb.
  • Apex (inlet): clavicle anterior, upper scapular border posterior, outer border of 1st rib medial.
  • Base: anterior fold = pec major; posterior fold = lat dorsi + teres major; chest wall medially.

Walls

  • Anterior: subclavius, pec minor, clavipectoral fascia, pec major.
  • Posterior: subscapularis, lat dorsi, teres major.
  • Medial: upper 4–5 ribs, intercostals, serratus anterior.
  • Lateral: coracobrachialis, biceps in bicipital groove.

Contents

Axillary artery & branches, axillary vein & tributaries, lymph vessels & nodes (limb, breast, trunk skin to umbilicus), brachial plexus, all in fat.

Clavipectoral fascia

  • Attached to clavicle, splits around pec minor, continues as suspensory ligament to the armpit floor.
  • Pierced by cephalic vein, thoracoacromial artery, lateral pectoral nerve.
Lab link: palpate anterior fold (pec major) and posterior fold (lat dorsi + teres major) with the arm abducted; find the deltoid tuberosity and acromion.
Last-minute

High-yield summary

  • Elbow = hinge; prox/distal radioulnar = pivot; wrist = ellipsoid; thumb CMC = saddle; MCP = condyloid; IP = hinge.
  • Elbow relations: ulnar nerve behind medial epicondyle; median nerve + brachial artery anterior.
  • Anular ligament: three attachments (capsule lower, capsule posterior, radial collateral) and not on the radius.
  • Posterior dislocation most common; aspirate at back beside olecranon.
  • Wrist cavity is separate from DRUJ; articular disc separates them. No wrist rotation; abduction < adduction.
  • Nerves: trapezius CN XI · lat dorsi thoracodorsal · rhomboids/levator dorsal scapular · pecs lateral+medial (minor: medial) · serratus long thoracic · deltoid + teres minor axillary · supra/infraspinatus suprascapular · teres major lower subscapular · subscapularis upper+lower subscapular.
  • Abduction: supraspinatus 0–15°, deltoid 15–90°, serratus + trapezius rotate scapula beyond 90°.
