Bones of the Lower Limb

Bony pelvis & hip bone

Pelvis

  • 4 bones: 2 hip bones (lateral/anterior walls) + sacrum & coccyx (posterior wall).
  • Hip bone = ilium + ischium + pubis, meeting at the acetabulum.
  • Articulate at symphysis pubis (front) and sacroiliac joints (back).

Ilium

  • Crest, ASIS/AIIS/PSIS/PIIS, tubercle of crest.
  • Gluteal surface: anterior, posterior, inferior gluteal lines (all end at greater sciatic notch).
  • Inner: auricular surface, iliac tuberosity, iliac fossa, arcuate line.

Ischium & pubis

  • Ischial spine separates greater (above) from lesser (below) sciatic notch.
  • Ischial tuberosity: sitting + muscle attachment.
  • Pubis: body, crest, pubic tubercle, 2 rami, pectineal line.
  • Obturator foramen = largest foramen in skeleton.
Pelvic brim: pectineal line + arcuate line. Above = false (greater) pelvis, part of abdomen (ileum, sigmoid colon, supports gravid uterus after month 3). Below = true (lesser) pelvis (inlet, cavity, outlet; bladder, uterus, ovaries), floored by the pelvic diaphragm.

Sacrum

  • 5 fused vertebrae; articulates with L5, coccyx and both iliac bones (SI joints).
  • Ala, promontory, anterior and posterior sacral foramina, sacral canal ending at the sacral hiatus.

Coccyx

  • 4 fused vertebrae, no vertebral canal.
  • Cornua articulate with sacral cornua.

Acetabulum

  • Deep fossa from ilium, ischium and pubis; receives femoral head.
  • Acetabular notch (inferior) transmits vessels and nerves.
Position: ASIS and front of pubic symphysis lie in the same vertical plane; the pelvic inlet faces anteriorly.

Leg: tibia & fibula

Tibia (medial, weight-bearing)

  • Medial condyle larger (groove for semimembranosus); lateral condyle has fibular facet.
  • Intercondylar eminence.
  • Tibial tuberosity: upper smooth part = patellar ligament; lower part subcutaneous.
  • Sharp anterior border; soleal line on posterior surface.
  • Distal: medial malleolus, fibular notch, articulates with talus.

Fibula (lateral, slender)

  • Not weight-bearing.
  • Head and apex articulate with lateral tibial condyle (proximal tibiofibular joint).
  • Medial interosseous border → interosseous membrane.
  • Distal: lateral malleolus (subcutaneous), malleolar fossa, grooves for fibular tendons.

Foot

7 tarsals→5 metatarsals→14 phalanges
  • Tarsals ossify from before birth until the 5th year: talus, calcaneus, navicular, cuboid, 3 cuneiforms.
  • Talus: only tarsal articulating with tibia and fibula (ankle); head, neck, body; sulcus tali.
  • Calcaneus: largest, heel; Achilles tendon on middle part of posterior surface; sustentaculum tali (groove for flexor hallucis longus); calcaneal sulcus + sulcus tali = tarsal sinus; articulates with cuboid in front.
  • Navicular: boat-shaped, medial; tuberosity for tibialis posterior tendon.
  • Cuboid: articulates with calcaneus, lateral cuneiform and lateral 2 metatarsals; groove for fibularis longus tendon.
  • Cuneiforms: articulate with navicular behind and medial 3 metatarsals in front.
  • Metatarsals: base, shaft, head. Phalanges: 2 in the big toe, 3 in each other toe.

Hip, Sacroiliac & Foot Joints

Hip joint

Type & surfaces

  • Synovial ball-and-socket (like shoulder, but more stable and less mobile).
  • Femoral head (more than a hemisphere, faces up, medially, forward) with acetabulum, covered by hyaline cartilage except the fovea.
  • Acetabulum: horseshoe lunate surface, non-articular fossa (fat pad), deepened by acetabular labrum; inferior notch bridged by transverse acetabular ligament (forms a foramen for vessels/nerves).

Capsule & synovium

  • Medial: acetabular margin + transverse ligament.
  • Lateral: anteriorly to intertrochanteric line; posteriorly halfway along the neck.
  • Fibres reflect up the neck as retinacula carrying vessels to head and neck.
  • Synovium lines capsule, covers intracapsular neck, ensheathes ligament of head and fat pad.
  • May bulge between pubofemoral and iliofemoral ligaments as the psoas (iliopsoas) bursa.

Relations

  • Anterior: iliopsoas, pectineus, rectus femoris.
  • Inferior: obturator externus tendon.
  • Superior: gluteus minimus, piriformis.
  • Posterior: obturator internus, gemelli, quadratus femoris, sciatic nerve.

Blood supply

  • Head and intracapsular neck: trochanteric anastomosis, mainly medial and lateral circumflex femoral arteries (plus superior and inferior gluteal).
  • Obturator artery branch in the ligament of head: important in young children.

Nerve supply

  • Hilton’s law: nerves to muscles crossing a joint also supply the joint.
  • Femoral (mainly), obturator, superior gluteal, nerve to quadratus femoris.
  • Hip pain is referred to the knee and front/medial thigh (obturator posterior division, femoral).
Stability depends on deep head-acetabulum fit, extracapsular ligaments and surrounding muscles; some movement is sacrificed for strength.

Clinical anatomy

Congenital dislocation

  • 1–2 per 1000; females > males; may be bilateral.
  • Shallow acetabulum and altered femoral head angle; limb shorter.
  • Ortolani (reduction: flexion + abduction, clunk) confirms; Barlow (dislocation: flexion + adduction).

Traumatic dislocation

  • Rare; occurs with hip flexed and adducted (dashboard injury; may fracture posterior acetabular rim).
  • Head goes posteriorly onto gluteal surface of ilium; sciatic nerve at risk.
  • Limb flexed, adducted, medially rotated, shortened; positive Trendelenburg sign (dipping/waddling gait).

Sacroiliac joint

  • Synovial joint (often ossifies with age).
  • Ligaments: anterior SI, interosseous SI (strongest), posterior SI, sacrotuberous, sacrospinous.
  • Sacrospinous: triangular, apex on ischial spine, base on sacrum and coccyx margins.
  • Sacrotuberous: triangular, superficial to sacrospinous; from PSIS, sacrum, coccyx to ischial tuberosity.
  • Together they convert the greater and lesser sciatic notches into foramina and prevent upward tilting of the sacrum.

Quick revision

High-Yield Summary

Numbers

  • Femoral neck angle 125°
  • Sacrum 5, coccyx 4 vertebrae
  • Tarsals 7, phalanges 14
  • Tarsal ossification complete by 5 years

Superlatives

  • Strongest hip lig.: iliofemoral; weakest: ischiofemoral
  • Strongest SI lig.: interosseous
  • Largest sesamoid: patella
  • Largest foramen: obturator
  • Largest tarsal: calcaneus

Pairs to remember

  • ACL ← lateral condyle; PCL ← medial condyle
  • Hyperextension ← iliofemoral
  • Medial rotation ← ischiofemoral
  • Ligament of head ← obturator artery
  • Hip pain → knee (Hilton’s law)
