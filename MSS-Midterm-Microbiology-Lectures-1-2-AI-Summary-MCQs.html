Lecture 1

Bacterial infections of the skin

Skin microbiome

  • Microbes live in the stratum corneum and hair follicles.
  • Predominant: S. epidermidis (harmless on skin, but infects prosthetic valves/joints).
  • Anaerobes (Propionibacterium, Peptococcus) sit in deep dermal follicles; Propionibacterium causes acne. C. albicans is also normal flora.

Depth of infection

Follicle: folliculitis→Furuncle (boil)→Carbuncle (multiple follicles)

Stratum corneum: impetigo→Epidermis: ecthyma→Superficial dermis + lymphatics: erysipelas→Dermis/superficial fascia: cellulitis→Deep fascia: necrotizing fasciitis
Also: hidradenitis suppurativa = apocrine glands (axilla, groin). Cutaneous abscess = pus in dermis/subcutis.

S. aureus

  • Clusters; colonizes anterior nares (10–40%).
  • Biofilm, capsule, Protein A (binds Fc of IgG, blocks opsonization), coagulase, PVL, exfoliative toxins, superantigens.
  • SSSS: exfoliative toxin cleaves desmosomes; infants; blisters then scald-like desquamation; heals 7–10 days.
  • TSS: TSST-1 (about 90% of menstrual TSS), superantigen; fever, hypotension, macular rash then palm/sole desquamation, multiorgan failure.
Gram +catalase −

S. pyogenes (GAS)

  • Chains; β-hemolytic, Lancefield group A.
  • M protein (serotyping; degrades C3b; anti-phagocytic), C5a peptidase, streptokinase, hyaluronidase, DNases.
  • Toxins: Spe (erythrogenic) superantigen; streptolysin O (oxygen-labile, immunogenic) and S (stable, non-immunogenic).
  • Scarlet fever: pharyngitis + rash from upper chest, circumoral pallor, strawberry tongue.
  • Strep TSS: bacteremia + necrotizing fasciitis.
  • Drug of choice: penicillin.

Post-strep immune sequelae

  • Rheumatic fever (after pharyngitis): heart, joints, brain, erythema marginatum.
  • Acute post-strep glomerulonephritis (after pharyngitis or skin infection): hematuria, proteinuria.
  • Mechanism: cross-reaction of anti-M antibodies with host tissue.

S. aureus treatment

  • Only 5–10% penicillin-sensitive (penicillinase).
  • Methicillin, nafcillin, oxacillin, dicloxacillin resist β-lactamase.
  • MRSA: mecA gene gives PBP2a (PBP2’) that β-lactams cannot bind.
  • MRSA: vancomycin, teicoplanin, daptomycin. Alternatives: linezolid, minocycline, tigecycline, TMP-SMX.

Lecture 2

Anaerobes in skin & MSK infections

Oxygen and anaerobes

  • Anaerobes lack catalase and superoxide dismutase (SOD), so H₂O₂ and superoxide build up; oxygen also oxidizes enzyme sulfhydryl groups.
  • Obligate: die in O₂ (Clostridium, Bacteroides).
  • Aerotolerant: tolerate O₂, do not use it (Lactobacillus), grow evenly in broth.
  • Facultative: with or without O₂ (E. coli, S. aureus), not true anaerobes.

Normal flora and origin

  • Anaerobe : aerobe ratio: colon 1,000–10,000:1, mouth 100–1,000:1, vagina about 10:1.
  • Endogenous: mucosal barrier breached (trauma, surgery, ischemia) into low-redox tissue.
  • Exogenous: tetanus, botulism.
Clues to anaerobes: abscess, foul odor, crepitus (gas), necrosis, deep infection with poor perfusion, polymicrobial, Gram stain shows bacteria but routine aerobic culture is sterile.

Clinical conditions

Necrotizing fasciitis

  • Type I (~80%): polymicrobial (anaerobes + Gram − rods + Gram + cocci).
  • Type II: monomicrobial, Group A Strep or MRSA.
  • Fournier’s gangrene: perineum/genitals.

Diabetic foot

Neuropathy/PAD→Trauma, Charcot→Ulcer→Polymicrobial→Osteomyelitis

Sample: deep tissue biopsy/curettage; avoid superficial swabs.

Bites

  • Animal: P. multocida, C. canimorsus, oral anaerobes.
  • Human: Eikenella corrodens, Fusobacterium, Prevotella, Peptostreptococcus, S. aureus.

Ludwig’s angina

Rapid bilateral cellulitis of floor of mouth, from odontogenic infection; viridans streptococci + Prevotella, Fusobacterium, Porphyromonas, Peptostreptococcus.

Lab and treatment

  • Specimen: deep tissue or abscess aspirate; no superficial swabs; anaerobic transport medium, minimal air exposure.
  • Gram stain: PMNs = inflammation; mixed pleomorphic = anaerobes; branching rods = Actinomyces; spindle rods = Fusobacterium.
  • Culture: pre-reduced media, anaerobic, 35–37°C, 48 h to days; ID by MALDI-TOF MS.
  • Treatment: source control first (drain, debride); empiric metronidazole, β-lactam/β-lactamase inhibitor or carbapenem; clindamycin for skin/mouth and toxin suppression; cover aerobes in polymicrobial disease.
High-yield: no SOD/catalase = oxygen toxicity; alpha toxin = gas gangrene; B. fragilis capsule; Fusobacterium = Lemierre; sulfur granules = Actinomyces; deep tissue sampling; drain/debride first.
