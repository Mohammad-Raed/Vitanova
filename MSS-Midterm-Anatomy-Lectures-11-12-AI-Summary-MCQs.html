Lecture 11

The Hand

Palm skin & fascia

  • Thick, hairless, many sweat glands, flexure creases; no superficial fascia
  • Deep fascia thickens into flexor retinaculum and palmar aponeurosis

Flexor retinaculum (FR)

  • Roof of the carpal tunnel; carpals form the floor
  • Medial: pisiform + hook of hamate (PH); lateral: scaphoid + trapezium (ST)
  • Tunnel carries flexor tendons + median nerve

Crosses FR superficially (6)

  • 3 ulnar: ulnar artery, ulnar nerve, palmar cutaneous br. of ulnar
  • 3 palmar: palmar cutaneous br. of median, palmaris longus tendon, FCU (partly attached)

Guyon’s canal

  • Fascial band bridging ulnar nerve + artery, attached to pisiform
  • Site of ulnar nerve entrapment

Palmar aponeurosis

  • Triangular; apex attached to FR and receives palmaris longus
  • Base: four slips, each splits into superficial (skin) and deep (finger root) bands
  • Function: firm skin attachment → better grip, protects tendons

Lumbricals

  • Origin: FDP tendons (tendon-to-tendon); insert on extensor expansion
  • Action: flex MCP, extend IP (writing position)
  • Same action: both interossei → fine movements

Anatomical snuffbox

  • Distal to radial styloid. Lateral: APL + EPB; medial: EPL; floor: scaphoid + trapezium
  • Contents: radial artery, origin of cephalic vein, superficial radial nerve
  • Scaphoid tenderness (fracture) and radial pulse felt here

Palmar arches

  • Superficial arch = mainly ulnar artery (completed by superficial branch of radial); gives digital arteries
  • Deep arch = mainly radial artery (completed by deep branch of ulnar), ~1 cm proximal
  • Not palpable; Allen’s test checks hand arterial patency

Hand arteries & nerves flow

Ulnar a.→Guyon’s canal→superficial arch

Radial a.→snuffbox→between heads of 1st dorsal interosseous→deep arch

Median nerve

  • Enters via carpal tunnel, deep to FR
  • Recurrent muscular branch at lower FR border = “million dollar nerve” (thenar muscles + 1st lumbrical)
  • Skin: palmar side of lateral 3½ fingers + distal dorsal tips

Ulnar nerve

  • Enters anterior to FR, lateral to pisiform → superficial + deep branches
  • Superficial: palmaris brevis + skin of medial 1½ fingers
  • Forearm branches: palmar cutaneous (hypothenar skin), dorsal cutaneous (medial ⅓ of dorsum)

Radial nerve (superficial br.)

  • Skin of lateral ⅔ of dorsum + proximal phalanges of lateral 3½ fingers
  • Dorsal area is variable

Carpal tunnel syndrome

  • Median nerve compression: burning/pins and needles in lateral 3½ fingers + thenar weakness
  • Causes: thickened flexor synovial sheaths, carpal arthritis
  • Thenar eminence skin spared (palmar cutaneous branch passes over FR)
  • Treatment: longitudinal FR incision, kept to the medial side to protect the recurrent branch
  • Tinel tap median nerve at wrist → tingling
  • Phalen wrists fully flexed, backs of hands together ~60 s → tingling
High-yield: PH medial / ST lateral on FR · Guyon = ulnar a+n · lumbricals flex MCP/extend IP · median 5, ulnar 15 · palmaris brevis = superficial ulnar · deep arch radial, superficial arch ulnar · CTS spares thenar skin.

The Forearm

Anterior compartment

Nerve rule: all by median (and its anterior interosseous branch) except FCU and the medial half of FDP (ulnar). Blood: ulnar and radial arteries. Superficial group shares a common tendon from the medial epicondyle.

Arteries

Brachial→ends opposite neck of radius→Radial (lateral)Ulnar (medial)
  • Radial: under brachioradialis; at wrist lies between brachioradialis tendon (lateral) and FCR (medial) → pulse; then snuffbox → deep palmar arch
  • Ulnar: deep to flexors above, superficial between FCU and FDS tendons below; enters palm in front of FR, lateral to pisiform → superficial palmar arch
  • Ulnar gives the common interosseous artery → anterior + posterior interosseous (on the interosseous membrane; wrist anastomosis); plus recurrent branches for elbow anastomosis

Nerves

Median (C6–T1)

  • Cubital fossa: deep to bicipital aponeurosis, medial to brachial artery/biceps tendon
  • Passes through pronator teres, runs between FDS and FDP
  • Branches: muscular, articular, anterior interosseous, palmar cutaneous (over FR)
  • AIN: FPL, lateral FDP (index + middle), pronator quadratus

Ulnar

  • Behind medial epicondyle; enters forearm between two heads of FCU
  • Runs between FCU and FDP; at wrist superficial between FCU and FDS
  • Supplies FCU + medial half FDP; palmar and dorsal cutaneous branches

Radial

  • Pierces lateral intermuscular septum, enters cubital fossa between brachialis and brachioradialis/ECRL
  • Divides at lateral epicondyle: superficial (sensory, lateral to radial artery under brachioradialis) and deep
  • Deep = posterior interosseous n.: pierces supinator, winds round the neck of the radius

Posterior & lateral compartments

Nerve rule: all by the radial nerve. Brachioradialis, ECRL and anconeus come from the radial trunk itself; the rest by the posterior interosseous nerve. Superficial group: common tendon from the lateral epicondyle.
