Lecture 11

The Hand

Palm skin & fascia

  • Thick, hairless skin with many sweat glands and flexure creases. The underlying fibrofatty superficial tissue is firmly tethered by fibrous septa; it is not absent, but is less mobile than loose superficial tissue elsewhere
  • Deep fascia thickens into flexor retinaculum and palmar aponeurosis

Flexor retinaculum (FR)

  • Roof of the carpal tunnel; carpals form the floor
  • Medial attachments: pisiform and hook of hamate (PH mnemonic); lateral attachments: scaphoid tubercle and ridge of trapezium (ST mnemonic)
  • The carpal tunnel carries the median nerve and nine tendons: four flexor digitorum superficialis (FDS), four flexor digitorum profundus (FDP), and one flexor pollicis longus (FPL) tendon. Flexor carpi radialis has a separate compartment; flexor carpi ulnaris is outside the tunnel

Structures superficial to or attached near the flexor retinaculum

  • 3 ulnar: ulnar artery, ulnar nerve, palmar cutaneous branch of the ulnar nerve
  • 3 palmar: palmar cutaneous branch of the median nerve, palmaris longus tendon, and flexor carpi ulnaris (FCU), which attaches to the pisiform rather than traversing the carpal tunnel

Guyon’s canal

  • The ulnar canal (Guyon’s canal) is a passage near the pisiform and hook of hamate for the ulnar nerve and artery, superficial to the flexor retinaculum. It is separate from the carpal tunnel
  • Site of ulnar nerve entrapment

Palmar aponeurosis

  • Triangular; apex attached to FR and receives palmaris longus
  • Base: four slips, each splits into superficial (skin) and deep (finger root) bands
  • Function: firm skin attachment → better grip, protects tendons

Lumbricals

  • Originate from FDP tendons and insert into the radial sides of the extensor expansions; they link flexor and extensor mechanisms
  • Action: flex metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joints and extend interphalangeal (IP) joints, including proximal interphalangeal (PIP) and distal interphalangeal (DIP) joints—useful for the writing position
  • Same action: both interossei → fine movements

Course counting convention: the lecture groups five median-supplied muscles (three thenar muscles and lumbricals 1–2) and 15 ulnar-supplied muscles (palmaris brevis plus 14 deep-branch muscles). That total counts four palmar and four dorsal interossei. Many standard descriptions count only three palmar interossei; flexor pollicis brevis also has variable/dual supply. Use the named muscle and branch relationships rather than treating these totals as universal anatomy.

Anatomical snuffbox

  • Distal to radial styloid. Lateral tendons: abductor pollicis longus (APL) and extensor pollicis brevis (EPB); medial tendon: extensor pollicis longus (EPL); floor: scaphoid + trapezium
  • The radial artery crosses the floor; the cephalic vein and superficial radial nerve lie in the superficial roof. Distinguish these layers when describing the “contents”
  • Scaphoid tenderness (fracture) and radial pulse felt here

Palmar arches

  • Superficial palmar arch is mainly from the ulnar artery and is commonly completed by the superficial palmar branch of the radial artery; it gives digital arteries. Completeness and contributions vary
  • Deep palmar arch is mainly from the radial artery, commonly completed by the deep ulnar branch, and lies about 1 cm proximal to the superficial arch; anatomical patterns vary
  • The arches are not ordinarily palpable. The Allen test assesses collateral hand perfusion through the radial and ulnar arterial routes; it is not a complete test of every hand artery

Hand arteries & nerves flow

Ulnar artery→Guyon’s canal→superficial arch

Radial artery→snuffbox→between heads of 1st dorsal interosseous→deep arch

Median nerve

  • Enters via carpal tunnel, deep to FR
  • The recurrent motor branch near the distal FR supplies the thenar muscles and is nicknamed the “million-dollar nerve”. The first and second lumbricals are supplied through median digital branches, not by the recurrent motor branch
  • Skin: palmar side of lateral 3½ fingers + distal dorsal tips

Ulnar nerve

  • Enters anterior to FR, lateral to pisiform → superficial + deep branches
  • Superficial: palmaris brevis + skin of medial 1½ fingers
  • Forearm branches: palmar cutaneous (hypothenar skin), dorsal cutaneous (medial ⅓ of dorsum)

Radial nerve (superficial branch)

  • Skin of lateral ⅔ of dorsum + proximal phalanges of lateral 3½ fingers
  • Dorsal area is variable

Carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS)

  • Median nerve compression: burning/pins and needles in lateral 3½ fingers + thenar weakness
  • Causes: thickened flexor synovial sheaths, carpal arthritis
  • Thenar eminence skin spared (palmar cutaneous branch passes over FR)
  • Surgical decompression releases the flexor retinaculum. The recurrent median motor branch has important anatomical variants, so a simple “always incise medially” rule is not an adequate surgical guide
  • Tinel tap median nerve at wrist → tingling
  • Phalen wrists fully flexed, backs of hands together ~60 s → tingling
High-yield: PH medial / ST lateral on FR · Guyon’s canal = ulnar artery and nerve · lumbricals flex MCP/extend IP · median 5 / ulnar 15 is the course counting convention, with anatomical exceptions noted above · palmaris brevis = superficial ulnar · deep arch radial, superficial arch ulnar · CTS spares thenar skin.

The Forearm

Nerve abbreviations: AIN = anterior interosseous nerve (a branch of the median nerve); PIN = posterior interosseous nerve (the continuation of the deep radial branch after passing through supinator). C and T in root ranges mean cervical and thoracic spinal nerve levels.

Anterior compartment

Nerve rule: all by median (and its anterior interosseous branch) except FCU and the medial half of FDP (ulnar). Blood: ulnar and radial arteries. Superficial group shares a common tendon from the medial epicondyle.

Arteries

Brachial→ends opposite neck of radius→Radial (lateral)Ulnar (medial)
  • Radial: under brachioradialis; at wrist lies between brachioradialis tendon (lateral) and FCR (medial) → pulse; then snuffbox → deep palmar arch
  • Ulnar: deep to flexors above, superficial between FCU and FDS tendons below; enters palm in front of FR, lateral to pisiform → superficial palmar arch
  • Ulnar gives the common interosseous artery → anterior + posterior interosseous (on the interosseous membrane; wrist anastomosis); plus recurrent branches for elbow anastomosis

Nerves

Median (C6–T1)

  • Cubital fossa: deep to bicipital aponeurosis, medial to brachial artery/biceps tendon
  • Passes between the humeral and ulnar heads of pronator teres, then runs between FDS and FDP
  • Branches: muscular, articular, anterior interosseous, palmar cutaneous (over FR)
  • AIN: FPL, lateral FDP (index + middle), pronator quadratus

Ulnar

  • Behind medial epicondyle; enters forearm between two heads of FCU
  • Runs between FCU and FDP; at wrist superficial between FCU and FDS
  • Supplies FCU + medial half FDP; palmar and dorsal cutaneous branches

Radial

  • Pierces lateral intermuscular septum, enters cubital fossa between brachialis and brachioradialis/ECRL
  • Divides at lateral epicondyle: superficial (sensory, lateral to radial artery under brachioradialis) and deep
  • The deep branch passes through supinator around the proximal radius and continues beyond it as the posterior interosseous nerve (PIN)

Posterior & lateral compartments

Nerve rule: all by the radial nerve. Brachioradialis, extensor carpi radialis longus (ECRL), and anconeus receive branches from the radial nerve before its terminal division. Extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB) and supinator commonly receive the deep branch before it emerges as the posterior interosseous nerve (PIN); most other extensors receive PIN branches. The common extensor tendon arises from the lateral epicondyle, but brachioradialis and ECRL arise more proximally from the lateral supracondylar region.
