Lecture essentials

1–2 · Musculoskeletal development

  • Somite sclerotome → vertebrae and ribs. Myotome → skeletal muscle. Dorsal rami supply epaxial back muscles. Ventral rami supply hypaxial body wall and limb muscles.
  • Vertebral resegmentation joins adjacent sclerotome halves. Annulus fibrosus comes from sclerotome and nucleus pulposus from notochord.
  • Limb bones and connective tissue come mainly from lateral plate mesoderm, limb muscle from migrating somitic cells. Skull vault forms mainly by intramembranous ossification, skull base mainly by endochondral ossification.
  • Apical ectodermal ridge drives proximal-to-distal outgrowth. Zone of polarizing activity patterns the thumb-to-little-finger axis. Lower limb rotates medially. Failure of normal digit separation can cause syndactyly.

3–4 · Gluteal region, thigh and knee

  • Piriformis divides the greater sciatic foramen. Superior gluteal bundle passes above it, sciatic nerve and inferior gluteal bundle usually pass below.
  • Anterior cruciate ligament limits anterior tibial translation. Posterior cruciate ligament limits posterior tibial translation. Medial collateral ligament resists valgus and attaches to the medial meniscus. Lateral collateral ligament resists varus and is separate from the lateral meniscus.
  • Knee locks in terminal extension. Popliteus initiates unlocking. Pes anserinus from anterior to posterior is sartorius, gracilis, semitendinosus.

5–6 · Lumbar plexus and femoral region

  • Lumbar plexus is mainly L1–L4, within psoas major. Femoral and obturator nerves are L2–L4. Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve is L2–L3.
  • Femoral triangle borders are inguinal ligament, sartorius and adductor longus. Lateral to medial: nerve, artery, vein, femoral canal. The nerve lies outside the sheath.
  • Adductor canal carries femoral artery and vein and saphenous nerve. Only the vessels pass through the adductor hiatus into the popliteal fossa.

7 · Sacral plexus

  • Sacral plexus is L4–S4 on anterior piriformis. Sciatic nerve is L4–S3 and contains tibial and common fibular divisions. Use the motor and sensory table above to trace their branches.

8–9 · Leg compartments and popliteal fossa

  • Extensor retinacula hold anterior tendons down. Fibular retinacula hold longus and brevis behind the lateral malleolus. The flexor retinaculum roofs the tarsal tunnel behind the medial malleolus.
  • Tarsal tunnel, anterior to posterior: tibialis posterior, flexor digitorum longus, posterior tibial vessels, tibial nerve, flexor hallucis longus.
  • Popliteal fossa borders: biceps femoris superolaterally, semimembranosus/semitendinosus superomedially, gastrocnemius heads inferiorly. Central contents from superficial to deep: tibial nerve, vein, artery. Common fibular nerve follows the lateral border.

10 · Vessels and pulses

  • External iliac → femoral below inguinal ligament → popliteal beyond adductor hiatus. Anterior tibial continues as dorsalis pedis. Posterior tibial gives rise to medial and lateral plantar arteries. Fibular artery usually branches from posterior tibial.
  • Femoral pulse at midinguinal point. Popliteal pulse deep in fossa with knee flexed. Posterior tibial pulse behind medial malleolus. Dorsalis pedis pulse just lateral to extensor hallucis longus tendon.
  • Great saphenous passes anterior to medial malleolus and drains into femoral vein. Small saphenous passes behind lateral malleolus and usually drains into popliteal vein. Genicular anastomoses provide collateral flow around knee.

11 · Localizing nerve injuries

  • With foot drop, check eversion and inversion as well as dorsiflexion. Weak dorsiflexion plus weak eversion suggests common fibular involvement. Weak dorsiflexion with preserved eversion suggests deep fibular involvement.
  • Test first web space, other dorsal skin, lateral border, medial border and sole separately. The skin patch and weak movement together localize a lesion better than either alone.
