Lecture 1

Skin Pharmacology: Psoriasis

Disease basics

  • Chronic, T-cell–mediated inflammatory skin disease; red plaques with silvery scales.
  • Whites ≈3% (~3× higher risk); Africans ≈1%.
  • Psoriasis vulgaris ≈80% of cases.
  • Diagnosis is clinical; no laboratory test.

Vs. RA

  • Psoriasis: localized, T-cells only.
  • RA: systemic, T and B cells.
  • Severe cases → psoriatic arthritis (metalloproteases erode cartilage).

Associations

  • Untreated: ↑ skin cancer risk.
  • Check metabolic syndrome: hyperlipidemia, hyperglycemia, CVD.
  • Flares: stress, smoking (free radicals), β-blockers.

Pathophysiology

Trigger→Dendritic cells→Naive T → pathogenic T→IL-17, IL-22, IL-23, TNF-α→Keratinocyte overgrowth

Management

  • Reduce stress, moisturize, UV phototherapy.
  • Topical = mild–moderate (no systemic effects); oral = moderate–severe or psoriatic arthritis.
  • Ointment (oily) absorbs better than cream/lotion.

Lecture 2

Anti-Rheumatoid Drugs

  • RA: chronic autoimmune disease, no cure. Goals: remission/low activity, prevent joint damage, preserve function.
  • Pathogenesis: dendritic cell → CD4⁺ T-cell → IL-17, TNF-α, IL-15/18 (↓ collagen), RANKL (bone erosion), B-cell/plasma cell autoantibodies.
  • Approach: drugs + joint stress reduction + physio/occupational therapy + surgery if needed.

NSAIDs

Symptomatic only; no prevention of joint damage. COX-2 selective for less GIT toxicity. Pain relief in hours; full effect 2–4 weeks.

Corticosteroids

Rapid; bridge therapy until DMARDs work; oral or intra-articular.

DMARDs

The only class that slows joint damage. Start early, at diagnosis; do not wait for X-ray changes.

Lecture 3

Muscle Relaxants

  • Skeletal muscle is voluntary, controlled by the somatic nervous system. At the NMJ, ACh binds nicotinic receptors → Na⁺ entry, depolarization, Ca²⁺ release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum → contraction.
  • Two groups: neuromuscular blockers (act at NMJ) and spasmolytics (act in the CNS).

Nondepolarizing (antagonists)

  • Cisatracurium, pancuronium, rocuronium, vecuronium (replaced tubocurarine).
  • Competitively block nicotinic receptors without stimulating them.
  • IV (rarely IM); poor membrane penetration, no BBB crossing.
Face/eyes→Fingers/limbs/neck/trunk→Intercostals→Diaphragm
Recovery is in reverse order.

Depolarizing: succinylcholine

  • The only depolarizing blocker; resists cholinesterase.
  • Phase I: persistent depolarization, fasciculations.
  • Phase II: channel closes, resistance to depolarization, flaccid paralysis.
  • Rapid onset → rapid intubation.
  • AEs: hyperthermia (treat with dantrolene: blocks Ca²⁺ release from SR), apnea (diaphragm), hyperkalemia.
DrugType / mechanismUse / adverse effects
OrphenadrineCentral muscle relaxant; also anticholinergic + antihistaminicShort-term painful strains/sprains. Dry mouth, blurred vision, constipation, urinary retention (muscarinic block); drowsiness (antihistaminic)
BaclofenGABAB agonist; inhibits excitatory reflex transmission in the spinal cordSpasticity (MS, spinal cord disease). Drowsiness, weakness, confusion; abrupt withdrawal is dangerous
High-yield: Muscle spasm (painful, musculoskeletal) ≠ spasticity (↑ tone, upper motor neuron). The drugs are not interchangeable: orphenadrine for strains, baclofen for spasticity.
