# Content refinement: Anatomy lectures 7–12

These edits follow the user's new authorization to improve summary content and correct confirmed quiz ambiguities. They preserve permanent lecture identities, filenames, section anchors, question counts, and the original quiz implementations. The 7–8 HTML file retains its CRLF line endings.

## Summary changes

### Lectures 7–8: joints, pectoral/scapular region, and axilla

- Expanded the joint-movement table into full movement names and defined carpometacarpal, metacarpophalangeal, interphalangeal, and distal radioulnar joint abbreviations before subsequent shorthand.
- Distinguished the radial-head surface articulating with the capitulum from the radial-head circumference at the proximal radioulnar joint.
- Explained that the course's 170°/167° carrying-angle values use the obtuse supplementary-angle convention, corresponding to about 10°/13° of lateral deviation. They are not universal individual measurements.
- Clarified that pronation and supination require coordinated proximal **and** distal radioulnar movement, whereas the elbow hinge produces flexion/extension.
- Replaced “grabbing” as a definition of thumb opposition with pad-to-pad contact and rotation toward the other fingertips.
- Distinguished normally separate joint cavities from possible pathological communications.
- Removed unsupported examination guarantees; retained “Handwritten” material as attributed course emphasis rather than externally verified instructor predictions.
- Explained the SITS mnemonic in the correct order, gave the full cranial-nerve abbreviation, and defined cervical/thoracic level notation.
- Described scapular winging as posterior prominence and impaired upward rotation rather than a fixed “laterally and posteriorly” displacement or absolute inability in every case.
- Clarified overlapping abduction muscle activity rather than abrupt on/off changes at 15°/90°.
- Corrected the long-head triceps border of the triangular space to lateral and distinguished the axilla's narrow bony lateral wall from adjacent arm muscles.
- Expanded pectoralis/latissimus shorthand and the muscle acronyms used for thumb movement.

### Lectures 9–10: arm, cubital fossa, and brachial plexus

- Expanded abbreviated nerve-root notation, extensor carpi radialis longus, blood pressure, and compressed plexus trunk/division/cord labels.
- Clarified that musculocutaneous injury weakens rather than abolishes elbow flexion because radial-innervated brachioradialis and a common radial contribution to brachialis remain.
- Replaced blanket “no branches in the arm” claims with “no major muscular or cutaneous branches,” allowing small vascular/articular branches.
- Clarified the radial nerve's lateral relationship and division at the cubital region.
- Replaced the misleading claim that the median cubital vein is the usual route for cardiac catheterisation with its ordinary venipuncture/peripheral-access role.
- Distinguished roots emerging between scalene muscles from trunks formed farther along the plexus, avoiding a universal “behind the subclavian artery” relation.
- Marked arterial branching and superficial venous patterns as usual rather than invariant.
- Clarified that the axillary sheath surrounds the artery and plexus cords, not the vein.
- Explained variable root contributions and the embryological anterior/posterior division pattern without implying that every posterior-division nerve only extends a joint.
- Identified six lymph-node groups as the lecture's classification, which counts infraclavicular nodes separately; many references instead use five main axillary groups.

### Lectures 11–12: hand and forearm

- Replaced “no superficial fascia” in the palm with a description of firmly tethered fibrofatty superficial tissue.
- Defined the flexor-retinaculum attachment mnemonics and named the nine carpal-tunnel tendons; separated flexor carpi radialis/ulnaris routes from tunnel contents.
- Distinguished Guyon's canal from the carpal tunnel and clarified the layers of the anatomical snuffbox.
- Expanded lumbrical MCP/IP actions and proximal/distal interphalangeal abbreviations; explained the PAD/DAB mnemonics.
- Made the four-palmar-interossei/median-five/ulnar-fifteen totals explicitly a course counting convention, with common three-palmar-interossei descriptions and variable/dual flexor pollicis brevis supply noted.
- Corrected the recurrent median motor branch description: lumbricals 1–2 receive median digital branches rather than the recurrent branch.
- Described palmar-arch completion as a common pattern with variants, and the Allen test as a collateral-perfusion assessment rather than proof of every artery's patency.
- Replaced a universal surgical-incision shortcut with the purpose of decompression and the importance of recurrent-branch variants.
- Distinguished the deep radial branch before/within supinator from the posterior interosseous continuation after it; identified ECRB/supinator innervation and superficial-origin exceptions.
- Clarified that the median nerve passes between the two heads of pronator teres, and expanded forearm muscle/nerve acronyms where used.

## Related quiz corrections

Some changes affect only explanations; others narrow a course-dependent stem or remove an ambiguous distractor. The exact old/new records, original groups, zero-based indexes, original canonical IDs, and newly generated fingerprints are recorded in [CONTENT_ANATOMY_LATE_QUIZ_CHANGES.json](CONTENT_ANATOMY_LATE_QUIZ_CHANGES.json) for the parent task's reviewed migration.

Direct attempts to access NCBI Bookshelf and TeachMeAnatomy returned a network-proxy CONNECT 403. Therefore these changes are **not** represented as an exhaustive external specialist review. The basic source checks above do not independently verify every advanced nerve variant, course-specific count, or procedural anatomy statement. Midcarpal classification, intrinsic-muscle counting conventions, root ranges, and examination exclusions remain explicitly distinguished as course-dependent points; the original slide/atlas source should be reviewed by the course instructor if examination-specific wording is essential.
