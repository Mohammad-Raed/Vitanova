## Current item records

### pp-mss-mid-0001

Which structure is posterior, rather than superior, to the glenohumeral (shoulder) joint?

Exam answer: Teres minor. Teres minor is a posterior relation. The supraspinatus tendon, subacromial bursa and coracoacromial arch are superior. The original negative stem was clarified without changing the tested relationship.

### pp-mss-mid-0002

The proximal radioulnar joint is which type of joint?

Exam answer: Synovial pivot. The radial head rotates within the annular ligament and radial notch, allowing pronation and supination at a pivot joint.

### pp-mss-mid-0003

A 7-year-old boy has a supracondylar fracture of the distal humerus. If the median nerve is injured, which action may be impaired?

Exam answer: Thumb opposition. A sufficiently proximal median nerve lesion can impair thenar opposition. Anterior interosseous branch injury is also common in this fracture pattern; the wording specifies a median nerve lesion rather than implying every fracture abolishes opposition.

### pp-mss-mid-0004

A 40-year-old mechanic who frequently uses a screwdriver develops weak thumb and finger extension. Finger abduction and adduction are preserved, and cutaneous sensation is normal. The affected radial nerve branch passes through which muscle?

Exam answer: Supinator. The deep radial branch passes through supinator and continues as the posterior interosseous nerve. Its injury impairs finger/thumb extension without a cutaneous sensory deficit.

### pp-mss-mid-0005

Which statement about the quadrangular space is incorrect?

Exam answer: Its inferior border is formed by a muscle that laterally rotates the shoulder. Teres major forms the inferior border and medially rotates, adducts and extends the arm; it is not a lateral rotator.

### pp-mss-mid-0006

Which statement about the axillary artery is correct?

Exam answer: The subscapular artery usually arises from its third part. Pectoralis minor defines the three parts. The artery begins at the lateral border of the first rib and becomes the brachial artery at the inferior border of teres major; subscapular origin is usually from part three.

### pp-mss-mid-0008

Which structure is NOT a superficial structure associated with the roof of the cubital fossa?

Exam answer: Lateral axillary lymph nodes. The lateral axillary nodes lie in the axilla, not in the roof of the cubital fossa. The roof includes skin, superficial fascia and deep fascia, with superficial veins and cutaneous nerves.

### pp-mss-mid-0009

A 42-year-old man sustains a deep palmar laceration extending from the hypothenar toward the thenar eminence. Which movement helps assess median thenar motor function?

Exam answer: Active palmar abduction of the thumb. Palmar thumb abduction tests abductor pollicis brevis, supplied by the recurrent median branch. Specifying palmar abduction distinguishes this from radial-nerve-assisted thumb abduction in the plane of the palm.

### pp-mss-mid-0010

Which growth factor promotes mitogenesis, collagen synthesis and bone growth/remodeling?

Exam answer: Insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1). IGF-1 mediates many growth-promoting actions of growth hormone, including effects on connective tissue and bone. The recalled answer was retained; missing distractors were reconstructed.

### pp-mss-mid-0011

Which statement is true according to the recalled exam key?

Recalled exam answer: Renshaw cells are responsible for lateral inhibition. Scoring follows the supplied paper key.

### pp-mss-mid-0012

During the stretch reflex, activation of an agonist is accompanied by inhibition of its antagonist. Which principle describes this?

Exam answer: Sherrington’s reciprocal inhibition. Reciprocal inhibition through spinal inhibitory interneurons reduces antagonist activity during agonist activation. “Relax to an equal extent” was removed because exact equality is not implied.

### pp-mss-mid-0013

In a Pacinian corpuscle experiment, the first node of Ranvier is blocked while receptor transduction remains intact. What occurs?

Exam answer: The generator potential persists, but action potentials are absent. The graded generator potential arises at the receptor ending; action potentials require an excitable trigger region near the first node. The recalled spelling of Pacinian was corrected.

### pp-mss-mid-0014

How is low-dose methotrexate commonly used in active rheumatoid arthritis?

Exam answer: As an early conventional disease-modifying treatment when appropriate. Methotrexate is a conventional synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD), commonly used early in active disease when suitable. Selection depends on contraindications and clinical assessment.

### pp-mss-mid-0015

A patient stops long-term topical minoxidil used for androgenetic hair loss. What is the likely consequence?

Exam answer: Treatment-dependent hair growth is gradually lost and hair loss resumes. Minoxidil benefit generally requires continued use; cessation allows treatment-dependent gains to be lost over time. It does not itself imply permanent scarring.

### pp-mss-mid-0016

Why do conventional neuromuscular blocking drugs have little direct central nervous system action?

Exam answer: They penetrate the blood–brain barrier poorly. Their highly ionized structure limits blood–brain barrier penetration. Paralysis does not supply unconsciousness or analgesia.

### pp-mss-mid-0017

Peptostreptococcus is best described as which organism?

Exam answer: An anaerobic Gram-positive coccus. Peptostreptococcus refers to anaerobic Gram-positive cocci; the genus is bacterial, not fungal. Some historically grouped organisms have been reclassified.

### pp-mss-mid-0018

Which is an example of a wound resulting from a normal physiological event?

Exam answer: The umbilical wound after separation of the cord stump. Cord separation leaves an umbilical wound during normal neonatal healing. The term “physiological wound” is clarified by its context; the alternatives reflect pathological or traumatic injury.

### pp-mss-mid-0019

Which statement about typical molluscum contagiosum lesions is incorrect?

Exam answer: They are usually painful and umbilicated. Typical lesions are usually painless dome-shaped umbilicated papules. Inflamed lesions can become tender, so the distinction is about the usual presentation.

### pp-mss-mid-0020

Which statement about Becker muscular dystrophy is false?

Exam answer: The dystrophin gene is located at Xq19. The dystrophin gene is at Xp21, not Xq19. Reading-frame preservation commonly explains the milder Becker phenotype; reducing the distinction to point mutation versus deletion is inaccurate.

### pp-mss-mid-0021

Which bone pattern is characteristic of Paget disease of bone?

Exam answer: A mosaic pattern of lamellar bone with prominent cement lines. Disordered resorption and formation produce the characteristic mosaic arrangement and irregular cement lines. This is a histological pattern, not an infectious organism.

### pp-mss-mid-0022

Which statement about ankylosing spondylitis is false?

Exam answer: Approximately 90% of patients are rheumatoid factor positive. Ankylosing spondylitis is a seronegative spondyloarthritis; rheumatoid factor positivity is not a defining high-frequency feature. HLA-B27 is an association, not a standalone diagnosis.

### pp-mss-mid-0047

When can endogenous anaerobes cause infection in normally sterile tissue?

Exam answer: After barrier disruption with entry into a low-oxygen tissue environment. Barrier disruption after trauma, surgery or other damage can introduce anaerobes into poorly oxygenated sites. This does not mean every commensal anaerobe is always pathogenic.

### pp-mss-mid-0048

Spontaneous nontraumatic Clostridium septicum myonecrosis should prompt investigation for which associated condition?

Exam answer: An underlying malignancy such as colorectal cancer. C. septicum infection has an important association with colorectal malignancy and hematologic disease. The association warrants investigation; it is not proof of cancer in every case.

### pp-mss-mid-0049

Which human leukocyte antigen is associated with seronegative spondyloarthritis?

Exam answer: HLA-B27. HLA-B27 is a class I human leukocyte antigen association. It is not diagnostic by itself, and rheumatoid factor is not an HLA molecule.

### pp-mss-mid-0050

Which process contributes to age-related osteoporosis?

Exam answer: Reduced osteoblast proliferative and bone-forming capacity. Reduced bone formation contributes to age-related osteoporosis along with other nutritional, hormonal and mechanical factors. It should not be presented as the only mechanism.

### pp-mss-mid-0057

Which statement about normal resting skeletal muscle tone is false?

Exam answer: It is the pathological velocity-dependent increase in tone called spasticity. Spasticity is a pathological velocity-dependent increase in stretch-reflex activity, not the definition of normal tone. Passive mechanical properties also contribute to measured resistance.

### pp-mss-mid-0058

Rapidly adapting phasic receptors are particularly useful for detecting what?

Exam answer: Changes in a stimulus, such as movement. Phasic receptors respond strongly to changes and often to onset/offset. “Movement receptors” is a functional description in this context, not a claim that every mechanoreceptor has identical adaptation kinetics.
