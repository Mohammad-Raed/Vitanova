Lecture 1 · Skull

Bones of the skull

8 cranial bones

Form the cranial cavity: frontal, 2 parietal, 2 temporal, occipital, sphenoid, ethmoid.

14 facial bones

Any bone not contributing to the cranial cavity: 2 nasal, 2 maxillae, 2 zygomatic, mandible, 2 lacrimal, 2 palatine, 2 inferior conchae, vomer.
Cavities to know: cranialnasalorbitalear The nasal and orbital cavities are related to the anterior and middle cranial fossae (orbit and nasal roof sit at the same level as the anterior fossa floor).

Sutures and landmarks

Coronal frontal + both parietals
Sagittal two parietals
Lambdoid parietals + occipital
Squamous parietal + temporal
Bregma: coronal meets sagittal. Lambda: sagittal meets lambdoid. Pterion: lateral junction of frontal, parietal, sphenoid, temporal. Orbitomeatal plane: orients the skull in anatomical position (parallel to the ground).
Clinical link: The pterion is the weakest point of the lateral skull. The middle meningeal artery (enters via foramen spinosum) lies just deep to it, so a fracture here can cause an extradural haematoma.

Lab link: Foramen lacerum lies between the petrous apex and sphenoid; in life it is filled with cartilage and fibrous tissue, so only small vessels cross it. Stylomastoid foramen lies between styloid and mastoid processes: VII exits here and never enters the cranial cavity.

  • Conical: base anteriorly, apex posteriorly (optic canal). No posterior wall.
  • Superior orbital fissure: between lesser and greater wings; connects orbit to the middle cranial fossa.
  • Inferior orbital fissure: between greater wing and maxilla/palatine; leads to the pterygopalatine fossa.

Pterygopalatine fossa (PPF)

Boundaries: roof = greater wing of sphenoid; anterior = maxilla; posterolateral = pterygoid process; medial = perpendicular plate of palatine; floor = horizontal plate of palatine. Contents: maxillary nerve (V2), pterygopalatine ganglion (relay for lacrimal, nasal and oral glands), maxillary artery.

High-yield summary

  • 22 bones = 8 cranial + 14 facial.
  • Four sutures: coronal, sagittal, lambdoid, squamous. Pterion = weak point over the middle meningeal artery.
  • Sphenoid: wings, sella turcica, pterygoids, foramina rotundum, ovale, spinosum.
  • Ethmoid: cribriform plate, crista galli, perpendicular plate.
  • Petrous temporal: ear, internal acoustic meatus, carotid canal.
  • PPF is the junction box linking orbit, nose, mouth, pharynx and cranium.

Lecture 2 · Neonatal skull

Neonatal versus adult skull

Key differences

  • Large cranium relative to face (face grows through childhood).
  • Mastoid process absent at birth.
  • Mandible: two halves fuse at the symphysis menti by end of year 1; angle is obtuse.
  • Bones separated by unossified membranes: fontanelles.

Ossification

Intramembranous most flat bones: mesenchyme → ossification centres; leftover membranes become fontanelles and then sutures.

Endochondral passes through a hyaline cartilage model first.

Lecture 2 · TMJ

Temporomandibular joint

  • The only synovial joint of the skull (hinge/gliding); all others are fibrous.
  • Surfaces: head (condyle) of mandible with mandibular fossa and articular tubercle of temporal bone.
  • Articular disc (fibrocartilage) attached to capsule and head of mandible; divides joint in two.
  • Nerve supply: auriculotemporal and masseteric branches of V3.
Upper compartment: gliding (translation)·Lower compartment: hinge (rotation)

Opening starts with gliding, and with wide opening the head then rotates.
