Fascial compartments of the leg

Deep fascia of the leg continues from the fascia lata of the thigh and is anchored to the anterior and medial borders of the tibia. Two intermuscular septa (anterior and posterior) pass from its deep surface to the fibula. Together with the interosseous membrane they divide the leg into anterior, lateral and posterior compartments. The medial tibial surface is subcutaneous. Clinically, the transverse septum divides the posterior region into superficial and deep spaces, giving four fascial compartments in total.

Cross-section of the leg (left)
  • Anterior and posterior intermuscular septa attach to the fibula, separating the lateral compartment from the other two.
  • Interosseous membrane separates anterior from posterior compartment.
  • Distally the fascia thickens into the flexor, extensor and fibular retinacula.

Both fibular nerves come from the common fibular nerve. A lesion at the fibular neck weakens dorsiflexion and eversion together. Tibialis posterior and the main plantar flexors retain their tibial nerve supply, so the patient can still invert and plantar flex.

Retinacula (thickenings of deep fascia at the ankle)

They hold the long tendons close to the ankle and act as pulleys. Tendons beneath them sit in compartments lined by synovial sheaths.

Extensor retinacula (front of ankle)
  • Superior extensor: above the ankle, between the anterior borders of the distal tibia and fibula. Almost all structures pass beneath it.
  • Inferior extensor: Y-shaped band in front of the ankle joint. It is a double layer, so structures pass through it (some deep, some superficial).
