Overview

Objectives

A lymphocyte is built (receptor gene assembly), tested (functional receptor? dangerously self-reactive?) and finally switched on (only when a genuine danger signal accompanies antigen recognition).

  • Explain V(D)J recombination and the checkpoints of B- and T-cell development.
  • Describe central tolerance: receptor editing, thymic selection and AIRE.
  • Describe the four mechanisms of peripheral tolerance.
  • Explain the three-signal model, co-stimulation and anergy.
  • Outline TCR and BCR signalling and the IL-2 proliferation loop.
  • Explain how superantigens cause toxic shock, and apply this to a case.

  • IL-2 appears only as: signals 1 + 2 → IL-2 and proliferation; anergic T cell → no IL-2.

Hand notes
  • Maturation sites: B cell → bone marrow; T cell → thymus.
  • Receptors: B cell carries the BCR; T cell carries the TCR (α and β chains).
  • Checkpoint 1: functional receptors built? Development passes through pro and pre stages; failure → cell death.
  • Checkpoint 2: functional receptors confirmed → must not be self-reactive (no recognition of the body’s own tissue). Central tolerance = self-tolerance during lymphocyte development.
  • Checkpoint 3: a cell that passed central tolerance leaves for the peripheral lymphoid organs and becomes naive = mature lymphocyte that has not yet met antigen. Earlier lecture: CD28 on the T cell binds B7 (CD80/CD86) on the APC.
  • Central tolerance → primary organs. Peripheral tolerance → secondary organs.
  • Diverse repertoire: needed to recognise the huge number of different antigens brought by APCs.
  • Disease pairs (as listed on slide): RAG → failure of V(D)J recombination · BTK → X-linked agammaglobulinaemia · IL-2Rγ → X-linked SCID. Detail in module 3.

Primary lymphoid organs

Sites of lymphoid-cell development (lymphopoiesis): bone marrow (B lymphocytes) and thymus (T lymphocytes).

Secondary lymphoid organs

Sites of antigen exposure: spleen, lymph nodes, mucosa-associated lymphoid tissues.

Generating the repertoire: V(D)J recombination

Maturation

The adaptive immune system needs receptors for an enormous range of antigens, yet the genome has only a limited number of gene segments. The solution is that receptor genes are assembled from segments, not inherited whole. The segments are called V (variable), D (diversity) and J (joining), followed by a C (constant) region.

Key points ‼ مهم

  • Cutting and joining: V, D and J segments are cut and rejoined by RAG1/RAG2 (recombination-activating genes). The cutting and joining are random, which is the origin of diversity.
  • Junctional diversity: nucleotides are trimmed and added by TdT at each join. This is the biggest source of variety.
  • Order and exclusion: the heavy chain is rearranged first, then the light chain. Allelic exclusion means only one of the two alleles is expressed, so each cell has one specificity.
  • Scale: a few hundred segments generate more than 10¹¹ possible receptors before any antigen is met.
  • Same mechanism in T cells: the TCR is rearranged in the thymus.
Hand notes
  • V, D and J gene segments form clusters; this DNA region is expressed to give the receptor.
  • Limited number of gene segments; several mechanisms, V(D)J recombination among them, generate the diversity.
  • RAG1/RAG2 cut and join randomly → diversity (marked ‼).
  • Rejoining can be inaccurate → TdT trims nucleotides (nucleotide trimming) and adds nucleotides at the junctions. TdT is one of the few enzymes that add nucleotides without a template → random; DNA sequence differs from cell to cell (marked ‼).
  • Genetic rearrangement: heavy chain first, then light chain (marked ‼).
  • Constant (C) region = antibody part (Fab / Fc sketch). The T-cell receptor is not an antibody, so this part is not present on T cells.
  • Immunoglobulin structure (Fab, Fc) as reference.
Extra
  • TCR genes also contain constant segments (Cα, Cβ) and rearrange by the same V(D)J strategy. Fab/Fc organisation and the secreted form are specific to antibodies.
  • P-nucleotides: short palindromic additions after hairpin cleavage and repair. N-nucleotides: non-templated additions by TdT.

Clinical links (slide 4)

  • Defects: RAG mutations cause SCID or Omenn syndrome (severe combined immunodeficiency).
  • Diagnosis: each clone carries a unique rearrangement. This is the basis of clonality testing in lymphoma: a polyclonal pattern means a reactive process; a monoclonal peak means lymphoma.

Central tolerance

Central tolerance

B-cell central tolerance: edit, delete or silence

Immature B cells in the bone marrow test their surface IgM against self antigens. Most newly formed B cells are self-reactive; receptor editing rescues many.

SituationOutcome
No binding to selfCell matures and leaves to the periphery.
Strong, multivalent self bindingReceptor editing: RAG is re-expressed and a new light chain replaces the old one (κ → λ).
Editing failsDeletion by apoptosis in the marrow.
Weak, soluble self antigenAnergy (functional unresponsiveness): low surface IgM, short life, no response to antigen.
EscapeesControlled in the periphery by lack of T-cell help.
Hand notes
  • Slide title annotated with three outcomes: edit, delete, silence.
  • No binding → fine; cell goes to the peripheral lymphoid organs.
  • Strong binding → a problem, but not eliminated immediately; the chance given is receptor editing. Fault is usually in the light chain: RAG1/RAG2 are re-expressed and replace the kappa light chain with a lambda light chain.
  • Anergy = functional unresponsiveness.

Clinical

Defects in these checkpoints raise autoantibody risk. CD19 CAR-T “resets” B-cell tolerance in refractory lupus (trials since 2022). CAR-T = chimeric antigen receptor T cell.

T-cell development in the thymus

Maturation

Seeding: bone-marrow progenitors enter the thymus at the cortico-medullary junction.

Hand notes
  • Bone marrow makes immature progenitor cells → blood → thymus; entry at the corticomedullary junction (separates cortex from medulla); maturation begins there.
  • DN: no CD4/CD8 co-receptors yet. DP: both present. SP: only one remains (CD4⁺CD8⁻ or CD4⁻CD8⁺).
  • TCR = two chains (α, β) made at different times. β first (V(D)J) → pairs with the temporary pre-Tα to form the pre-TCR. Checkpoint tests that β works; failure → apoptosis.
  • DP: α chain made and paired with β → complete TCR. Key question: does it recognise MHC? (selection, next section).
  • SP: moves to the medulla for further selection on self-MHC; strong recognition of self-peptide–MHC → apoptosis.
  • Only ~2–5% of thymocytes survive; the rest undergo apoptosis.
  • γδ T cells branch off early; many home to epithelia (circled). Involution: output peaks in childhood, falls after puberty.
  • 22q11.2 deletion → thymic hypoplasia = DiGeorge syndrome. Complete athymia → thymus tissue implantation. Not covered in class; to be taken in the final lectures and mentioned in earlier lectures.

Thymic selection: the affinity model

Central tolerance

Fate follows affinity: how strongly the TCR binds self-peptide–MHC decides survival.

Binding to self-peptide–MHCOutcomeNote
NoneDeath by neglectAbout 90% of thymocytes.
WeakPositive selectionMHC restriction and the CD4 or CD8 decision.
IntermediateThymic TregFOXP3⁺ regulatory cells.
StrongNegative selectionApoptosis of dangerous clones.
Hand notes
  • CD4 / CD8 decision: TCR + CD8 binds MHC I (CD8 × MHC I = 8); TCR + CD4 binds MHC II (CD4 × MHC II = 8).
  • AIRE = Autoimmune Regulator; acts in medullary thymic epithelial cells (mTECs) → display insulin, thyroglobulin and other tissue proteins.
  • APECED = Autoimmune Polyendocrinopathy-Candidiasis-Ectodermal Dystrophy.
Exam point
  • AIRE loss → APECED (APS-1): endocrine autoimmunity (e.g. hypoparathyroidism), candidiasis, anti-cytokine antibodies.
  • FOXP3 loss → IPEX (immune dysregulation, polyendocrinopathy, enteropathy, X-linked): Tregs lost.
  • ~90% die by neglect; most of the rest are lost by negative selection → only ~2–5% leave the thymus.

Suppression by TregsInhibition of immune responses by Tregs.CTLA-4 strips B7 from dendritic cells; IL-2 consumption; IL-10 and TGF-β.

Clinical lessons

  • FOXP3 defect → IPEX; CTLA-4 haploinsufficiency → autoimmunity treated with abatacept (CTLA-4-Ig).
  • Sympathetic ophthalmia after eye injury: ignorance is broken when eye antigens are exposed.
  • Teplizumab (anti-CD3) delays type 1 diabetes by re-inducing tolerance.
Extra
  • CTLA-4 competes with CD28 for B7 (CD80/CD86) and strips B7 from dendritic cells, reducing signal 2.
  • One functional CTLA4 allele (haploinsufficiency) → weaker brake → autoimmunity. Abatacept (CTLA-4 fused to Ig) blocks B7 and restores the brake.

Co-stimulation links activation to danger

Activation
Hand note
  • The story is more than T-cell antigen recognition: a danger context is also required.
  • Resting dendritic cells express little B7, so self antigen induces anergy or Tregs.
  • Microbes change the picture: PAMPs act through Toll-like receptors and raise B7 and CD40 on dendritic cells.
  • Why adjuvants work: they supply the danger signal that a purified antigen lacks.
  • Two-way conversation: CD40L on activated T cells licenses the dendritic cell to make IL-12.
  • Later co-stimulators: ICOS, OX40 and 4-1BB sustain effector and memory cells.
  • Clinical: the CD28 superagonist TGN1412 caused a cytokine storm in a 2006 first-in-human trial; 4-1BB and CD28 domains power CAR-T cells.

High-yield revision summary

Checkpoints

  • Working receptor (pre-BCR / pre-TCR): failure → death; RAG, BTK, IL-2Rγ.
  • Not dangerously self-reactive: central tolerance.
  • Genuine danger: co-stimulation; failures of tolerance → AIRE, FOXP3, CTLA-4.
  • Primary organs ↔ central tolerance; secondary organs ↔ peripheral tolerance. Naive = mature, antigen-inexperienced.

V(D)J

  • RAG1/RAG2 cut and join randomly.
  • TdT junctional diversity = biggest source of variety.
  • Heavy chain first, then light; allelic exclusion → one specificity.
  • >10¹¹ receptors. RAG defect → SCID / Omenn. Unique rearrangement → clonality testing (monoclonal → lymphoma).

B cells

  • Pro-B (heavy chain) → pre-B (μ + surrogate light chain, BTK) → immature B (IgM) → mature naive B (IgM + IgD).
  • XLA: BTK, X-linked recessive, block at pre-B. Ibrutinib treats CLL.
  • No binding → leaves; strong multivalent → receptor editing (RAG, κ → λ); editing fails → deletion; weak soluble → anergy.

T cells

  • DN (TCR β + pre-Tα, pre-TCR checkpoint) → DP (α chain, self-MHC test) → SP (CD4 or CD8).
  • ~2–5% survive. γδ T cells branch early, home to epithelia. Involution after puberty. 22q11.2 → DiGeorge.

Affinity model

  • None → neglect (~90%) · weak → positive selection (CD4 ↔ MHC II, CD8 ↔ MHC I) · intermediate → thymic Treg (FOXP3) · strong → negative selection.
  • AIRE in mTECs: insulin, thyroglobulin; loss → APECED.

Peripheral tolerance

  • Anergy · Treg suppression (CTLA-4, IL-2 sink, IL-10, TGF-β) · deletion (Fas–FasL, BIM) · ignorance (eye, testis, brain).
  • IPEX; CTLA-4 → abatacept; sympathetic ophthalmia; teplizumab.

Three signals

  • Signal 1: TCR–peptide–MHC (anti-CD3, teplizumab). Signal 2: B7–CD28 (abatacept, belatacept). Signal 3: cytokines (anti-cytokines, JAK inhibitors).
  • Signal 1 alone → anergy; 1 + 2 → IL-2, proliferation; 1 + 2 + 3 → Th1/Th2/Th17/Treg.
  • B cells: BCR, then CD40L + cytokines.

Danger and co-stimulation

  • PAMP → TLR raises B7 and CD40; adjuvants supply danger.
  • CD40L licenses DC for IL-12. ICOS, OX40, 4-1BB sustain effector/memory cells.
  • TGN1412: cytokine storm. 4-1BB / CD28 domains power CAR-T.
