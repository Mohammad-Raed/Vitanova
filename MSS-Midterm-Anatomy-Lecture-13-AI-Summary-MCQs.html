Nerve 1

Median nerve

Course in brief

Lateral + medial cords (axilla)→Arm: lateral then crosses to medial of brachial artery→Forearm: between heads of pronator teres→Carpal tunnel→Hand
  • Supplies the anterior forearm except flexor carpi ulnaris (FCU) and the medial half of flexor digitorum profundus (FDP) (ulnar).
  • Anterior interosseous nerve (AIN): deep muscles of anterior forearm.
  • Palmar cutaneous branch: arises just before the flexor retinaculum.
  • Recurrent motor branch (RMB): supplies the thenar muscles. Palmar digital branches supply the 1st and 2nd lumbricals and sensation to the lateral 3½ fingers.

Injury at the elbow (supracondylar humeral fracture)

Motor
  • Pronators paralysed → forearm rests supinated.
  • Flexor carpi radialis (FCR) paralysed → wrist flexes weakly with ulnar deviation (FCU, ulnar nerve, is intact).
  • Flexor digitorum superficialis (FDS) and lateral half of FDP lost → on making a fist, index (and to a lesser extent middle) stay straight while ring and little flex: “Pope’s blessing”.
  • Flexor pollicis longus (FPL) lost → no flexion of thumb distal phalanx.
  • Thenar wasting → flat, ape hand; thumb adducted and laterally rotated; no opposition.
Sensory
  • Lateral ½ of palm + palmar lateral 3½ fingers.
  • Dorsal nail beds (distal dorsal) of the same fingers.
  • True total anaesthesia is smaller because of overlap.
Clarifier: the thumb is adducted because adductor pollicis (ulnar) still works; opposition is lost because opponens pollicis is gone.

Injury at the wrist

  • Thenar wasting, ape hand, thumb adducted/laterally rotated, no opposition.
  • Lumbricals 1 and 2 paralysed: when making a fist slowly, index and middle lag behind.
  • Forearm flexors are preserved: no loss of pronation, no adducted wrist, no loss of index/middle interphalangeal (IP) joint flexion.
  • Worst disability: loss of thumb opposition (pincer grip) plus lateral finger sensation.

Anterior interosseous nerve (AIN)

  • Pure motor (no sensory loss): FPL, lateral ½ FDP, pronator quadratus.
  • Seen in entrapment/compression, e.g. radial neck fracture per lecture notes.
  • OK sign fails: cannot flex thumb IP or index distal interphalangeal (DIP) joint → abnormal “pinch” sign.
Nerve 2

Radial nerve

Course in brief

  • Axilla: long + medial heads of triceps; posterior cutaneous nerve of arm.
  • Radial (spiral) groove: lateral + medial heads; lower lateral cutaneous nerve of arm and posterior cutaneous nerve of forearm. Then pierces to the anterior compartment (brachioradialis, extensor carpi radialis longus (ECRL) supplied before the split).
  • At the lateral epicondyle it splits: superficial branch (sensory) and deep branch (passes through supinator and continues as the posterior interosseous nerve, PIN; supplies extensor muscles).
LevelCauseFindings
AxillaCrutch pressure or another sufficiently proximal radial nerve lesionEverything lost: triceps, wrist/finger extensors → wrist drop and no elbow extension. Small sensory loss over posterior lower arm/forearm. Supination preserved (biceps).
Spiral grooveHumeral shaft fractureCompression during sleep (“Saturday night palsy”) can also affect this region. Wrist drop but elbow extension preserved (triceps branches leave earlier). Sensory loss: lower lateral arm, posterior forearm, dorsum of hand.
Posterior interosseousRadial neck fracture / radial head dislocationNo wrist drop (ECRL intact and strong enough). No sensory loss. Finger/thumb extension lost: hitch-hike sign fails.
Superficial radialStab woundPurely sensory: small variable anaesthesia over dorsum of hand and roots of lateral 3½ fingers.
Clarifier: wrist drop is really wrist flexion from unopposed flexors. It is disabling because you cannot grip firmly with the wrist flexed.
Nerve 3

Ulnar nerve

  • Forearm: FCU + medial ½ FDP. Gives palmar and dorsal cutaneous branches, then passes through Guyon’s canal.
  • Hand: the hypothenar muscles, lumbricals 3–4, interossei, adductor pollicis, and usually the deep head of flexor pollicis brevis; fixed muscle counts depend on counting conventions.
  • Common sites: behind the medial epicondyle, wrist lacerations, fracture of hook of hamate.
Motor
  • Claw hand: metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joints hyperextended and IP joints flexed, especially in the ring and little fingers (lumbricals lost).
  • Loss of finger abduction and adduction (interossei); hypothenar atrophy; no thumb adduction.
  • Paper test: cannot hold paper between fingers.
  • Froment’s sign: cannot adduct thumb, so patient flexes thumb IP (FPL, median) to hold paper.
Sensory
  • Anterior and posterior medial third of hand + medial 1½ fingers (and nail beds).
Clarifier (elbow vs wrist): clawing is worse at the wrist, because with an elbow lesion the medial FDP is also lost, so ring/little IP flexion is weaker. Elbow lesion also gives minimal weak wrist flexion with radial deviation (FCU lost). Index/middle claw less because their lumbricals are median.
Nerves 4 and 5

Musculocutaneous & axillary

Musculocutaneous

  • Rarely injured (deep to biceps); pierces coracobrachialis, runs between biceps and brachialis.
  • High lesion: biceps + coracobrachialis paralysed, brachialis weakened (dual supply with radial) → weak elbow flexion.
  • Sensory loss: lateral forearm.

Axillary

  • Posterior cord; passes behind surgical neck; supplies deltoid + teres minor.
  • Causes: crutch, downward humeral head dislocation, surgical neck fracture.
  • Deltoid wasting (rounded shoulder becomes bony, greater tuberosity palpable); abduction impaired (15–90°); teres minor loss not clinically obvious.
  • Sensory loss: lower half of deltoid (upper lateral arm).
