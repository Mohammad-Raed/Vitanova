Skin layers & key terms Basics

  • Epidermis: keratinocytes + melanocytes (pigment). Dermis: vessels, nerves. Subcutis: fat.
  • Premalignant: not yet invasive but can become malignant. Malignant: invades; metastasis = spread to nodes/organs (not equal in all tumours).
  • In situ: malignant cells confined to the epidermis, basement membrane intact.
  • Depth ladder: AK (lower epidermis) → SCC in situ (full thickness) → invasive SCC (through basement membrane into dermis).

Actinic keratosis Premalignant

Spot it: Rough sandpaper patch on sun-exposed skin.
  • Dysplastic lesion from UV DNA damage; hyperkeratosis makes it rough (sandpaper-like), <1 cm, tan-brown or red.
  • Fair skin (less melanin); face, arms, dorsum of hands; rises with age and sun.
  • Progression to SCC only 0.1–2.6%/year; most regress or stay stable.
  • Micro: atypia in the lower epidermis, hyper-/parakeratosis, basal hyperplasia or atrophy, solar elastosis (blue-gray elastic fibres).
  • Treatment: cryotherapy or topical agents (prevent progression or cosmetic).

Squamous cell carcinoma Malignant

Spot it: Scaly nodule that ulcerates on sun-damaged skin.
  • Common, sun-exposed sites of older adults; usually found small and resectable.
  • Causes: UV (huge mutation load); immunosuppression (transplant), tars/oils, chronic ulcers, burn scars, ionizing radiation.
  • In situ: sharp red scaly plaque; atypical cells at all epidermal levels, basement membrane intact.
  • Invasive: nodular scaly lesion that may ulcerate; atypical cells in dermis, well-differentiated (keratinizing) to anaplastic, dyskeratosis.
  • Worse prognosis: poor differentiation, deep invasion into fat, vascular/perineural invasion, ear or hair-bearing lip, arising in scars/ulcers or non–sun-exposed skin.

Basal cell carcinoma Malignant

Spot it: Pearly papule + telangiectasia; palisading nests with clefts.
  • Common, slow-growing, rarely metastasizes but destroys locally (bone/sinus invasion = “rodent ulcer”).
  • Sun-exposed sites, lightly pigmented people.
  • PTCH1 loss-of-function (the “brake”) → SMO stays active → constitutive Hedgehog signalling. TP53 mutations also common.
  • Gorlin syndrome: AD, inherited PTCH1 defect, familial BCC.
  • Micro: basaloid nests, peripheral palisading (fence-like nuclei), clefting artifact (nests separate from stroma).

Melanoma Malignant

Spot it: Changing, multi-coloured lesion with irregular border.
  • Melanocyte tumour; less common but worst prognosis; most are cured surgically if caught early.
  • UV damage; 5–10% hereditary; CDKN2A (p16 → RB, p14 → p53) in ~40% of familial cases.
  • Growth: lentiginous hyperplasia → dysplastic nevus → radial (in situ, pagetoid upward spread) → vertical (into dermis; nodule; metastatic potential).
  • Mutations: BRAF/RAS → telomerase → loss of p16 (vertical) → loss of p53/PTEN (metastasis).
  • Gross: black/brown/red/blue/gray variegation, irregular notched border. Micro: large cells, irregular nuclei, cherry-red nucleoli, brisk lymphocytes. Benign nevus matures (smaller deep cells); melanoma does not.
  • Prognosis: vertical growth, thick Breslow depth, mitoses, ulceration. Warning signs: change in colour/size, new lesion in adult, rapid growth, itch/pain, irregular borders.
  • Treatment: surgery; BRAF/KIT inhibitors and immune checkpoint inhibitors for advanced disease.
SCC = Sun + Scars + Suppression. BCC = Brake (PTCH1) broken, Barely metastasizes, Basaloid palisades.
