Abbreviations & basics

ssRNA / dsDNA = single-stranded RNA / double-stranded DNA · HHV = human herpesvirus · HSV = herpes simplex virus · VZV = varicella-zoster virus · HPV = human papillomavirus · HFMD = hand, foot and mouth disease · MMR = measles, mumps, rubella vaccine · SSPE = subacute sclerosing panencephalitis · CRS = congenital rubella syndrome · TAC = transient aplastic crisis · PRCA = pure red cell aplasia · PHN = postherpetic neuralgia · CNS = central nervous system.

Virus structure

  • Nucleic acid (DNA or RNA, never both) + protein capsid = nucleocapsid; some have a lipid envelope.
  • Symmetry: icosahedral, helical, complex.
  • Taxonomy: family → subfamily → genus → species (e.g. Picornaviridae → Enterovirus → Coxsackie).

Naked vs enveloped

Naked: Parvovirus B19 · HPV · Coxsackie A

Mnemonic: P-H-C = naked. Everything else here (measles, rubella, herpes, pox) is enveloped.

Measles

  • Airborne aerosols; one of the most contagious viruses (rubella is less).
  • Incubation 7–14 days. Prodrome: high fever + CCC (cough, coryza = runny nose, conjunctivitis); most contagious now.
  • Koplik’s spots (blue-grey with red base, buccal mucosa), then rash ~2 days later; spreads face → down incl. palms/soles.
  • Complications: pneumonia, otitis media, diarrhoea, encephalitis, SSPE (rare, fatal, ~6–10 years later).
  • Prevention: live MMR.

Rubella

  • Airborne; incubation 14–21 days; infectious 1 week before to 4 days after rash.
  • Postnatal: lymphadenopathy + rash; mild in children; adults: arthritis/arthralgia, encephalitis.
  • CRS: cataracts, deafness, heart defects, intellectual disability; risk highest in 1st trimester (<20 weeks).
  • Fetus infected only if the mother is non-immune and gets a new infection; IgG (from MMR or past infection) protects.

Parvovirus B19

  • Smallest DNA virus pathogenic to humans; respiratory droplets → viraemia → erythroid precursors in bone marrow.
  • Immunocompetent: asymptomatic, rash, arthralgia; child: slapped cheek.
  • Haemolytic disease (e.g. sickle cell): TAC – sudden halt of RBC production, ↓Hb, absent reticulocytes.
  • Immunocompromised: PRCA (chronic anaemia).
  • Fetus <20 weeks: hydrops fetalis (fluid in tissues/cavities, anaemia, myocarditis, heart failure) → death.

Roseola (HHV-6/7)

  • Herpesviruses with latency (in T cells). Age 4 months–3 years.
  • Abrupt high fever 3–5 days → rose-pink, non-itchy papules on back/neck → chest/limbs, sparing face and feet.
  • Fever may cause febrile seizures.
Clarifications: Rash direction: measles/rubella start on the face; chickenpox starts on the trunk. The lecture gives MMR at 12 and 18 months (measles slide adds preschool): follow your national schedule; MMR is live attenuated.

Herpesviruses

Large, enveloped dsDNA viruses with icosahedral capsid. Hallmark: latency (dormant in specific cells) and later reactivation, especially with stress or immunosuppression.

HSV-1 and HSV-2

  • Spread by direct contact with infected secretions/mucosa/broken skin. Primary infections often have fever, malaise, anorexia.
  • HSV-1: primary herpetic gingivostomatitis; herpes labialis (reactivation); herpetic whitlow (fingertip); keratitis; encephalitis.
  • HSV-2: genital herpes; meningitis and radiculopathy; neonatal herpes (delivery; skin, CNS or disseminated).
  • Both can affect the eye (keratitis, uvea, retina) and nervous system.

Varicella-zoster virus

  • Respiratory droplets or vesicle fluid. Respiratory tract → viraemia → disseminated rash → latency in dorsal root ganglia.
  • Chickenpox: macule → papule → vesicle → crust; successive crops so different stages at once (key diagnostic sign); starts on trunk then face/extremities.
  • Shingles: painful unilateral vesicular rash in one dermatome, burning/tingling prodrome; thoracic and trigeminal common.
  • Most common complication: PHN (long-lasting pain in the rash area).
