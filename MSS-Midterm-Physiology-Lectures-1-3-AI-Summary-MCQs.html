Lecture 1

Motor Units, Force & the NMJ

Motor unit

  • One motor neuron + all fibres it innervates
  • Each mammalian fibre gets only one synapse
  • Single unit = smallest force a muscle makes

Recruitment

  • Size principle (Henneman): smallest neuron first, largest last
  • Small neurons: high resistance → bigger EPSP (V = IR)
  • Order set by spinal mechanisms; least fatigable first
  • Asynchronous recruitment delays fatigue

4 ways to increase force

WayKey point
RecruitmentMore motor units; dominant in large muscles (legs)
Firing rateTwitches summate → tetanus; dominant in small muscles (hands)
Firing patternA short interval between impulses gives more force
Length–tensionMax active tension at optimal overlap (~2.0–2.3 µm); too short = filament interference, too long = poor overlap; passive tension rises with stretch

Neuromuscular junction (NMJ)

  • Huge (~1000 µm² vs 0.05 µm² central synapse); folded postsynaptic membrane, very dense nAChR
  • One motor-neuron AP always gives a muscle AP (large ACh release, many receptors, many voltage-gated Na⁺ channels) – no summation needed

Lecture 2

Structure & Contraction

Sarcomere (Z to Z)

  • A band: thick filaments (constant length)
  • I band: thin only; Z disk in middle
  • H zone: thick only; M line in centre
  • Contraction: I band and H zone shorten, A band stays

Proteins

  • Myosin (thick): actin-binding site + ATPase
  • Actin (thin): binding sites for myosin
  • Tropomyosin: covers binding sites
  • Troponin (T, I, C): Ca²⁺ binds TnC
  • Titin: elastic, M line to Z; nebulin: aligns actin

Excitation–contraction coupling

AP along sarcolemma & T-tubule→DHP receptor changes shape→RyR1 opens, Ca²⁺ out of SR→Ca²⁺ binds TnC→Tropomyosin moves→Cross-bridge cycling
Note: the DHP receptor acts as a voltage sensor, not a major Ca²⁺ entry route.

Cross-bridge cycle

ATP binds, myosin releases actin→ATP hydrolysed, head cocked→Binds actin (Ca²⁺)→Power stroke (Pi, ADP off)

Relaxation

  • AP stops; AChE degrades ACh
  • SERCA pumps Ca²⁺ into SR (calsequestrin stores it)
  • Ca²⁺ leaves troponin; tropomyosin re-covers sites
  • Titin / antagonist muscle restore length

ATP is needed for

  • Myosin ATPase (power stroke, detachment)
  • SERCA (Ca²⁺ reuptake)
  • Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase (repolarisation)
Rigor mortis: after death Ca²⁺ leaks out, heads bind actin, but no ATP means they cannot detach. Starts ~3–4 h, peaks ~12 h, ends as myosin heads break down.
Lecture 3

Touch & Muscle Receptors

Muscle spindle

  • Senses muscle length (proprioception)
  • Intrafusal fibres wrapped by sensory endings; innervated by γ-motoneurons
  • Ia: length + rate of change (dynamic)
  • II: static length
  • Stretch-sensitive channels: more stretch, more depolarisation

α–γ co-activation

  • α alone: muscle shortens, spindle goes slack, Ia silent
  • α + γ: spindle stays taut and responsive
  • Static γ: length sensitivity; dynamic γ: speed sensitivity

Coding

  • Rate coding: higher intensity = more APs/s
  • RA: fire at onset/offset; SA: fire throughout
  • SA1: smallest probe gives the highest rate
  • Location = which receptors are active

Acuity & pathways

  • Small receptive fields: best 2-point discrimination (fingertips, lips, tongue)
  • Cell body in dorsal root ganglion; afferents enter via dorsal root
  • Dermatome: skin supplied by one dorsal root (diagnostic)
  • Homunculus in postcentral gyrus; distorted by receptor density; right body maps to left cortex
Last look

High-Yield Summary

  • Size principle: small units first; hands use rate, legs use recruitment
  • NMJ: one AP always fires the muscle; MG (receptors), botulinum (release), organophosphates (AChE)
  • Contraction: DHP → RyR1 → Ca²⁺ → troponin C → tropomyosin moves; A band constant
  • ATP detaches myosin, powers SERCA and Na⁺/K⁺ pump; no ATP = rigor
  • Ia = length + rate; II = static; Ib = tension; α–γ co-activation keeps spindle taut
  • Meissner/Merkel: superficial, precise; Pacinian: vibration; Ruffini: stretch
