Axial skeleton

Vertebral column

  • Week 4: sclerotome mesenchyme surrounds the neural tube and notochord, forming segments: loose cranially, dense caudally.
  • Each vertebral body = dense (caudal) half of one sclerotome + loose (cranial) half of the sclerotome below it. So bodies are intersegmental; myotomes and nerves lie against the disc and intervertebral foramen (IVF).
  • Intervertebral disc (IVD): anulus fibrosus from dense sclerotome cells; nucleus pulposus from notochord remnants.
Clinical link: Scoliosis (lateral curvature) can be congenital: asymmetric fusion of vertebrae or a missing half (hemivertebra).

Primary centre (diaphysis, week 7)

  • Perichondrium lays periosteal bone collar
  • Nutrient artery enters; osteogenic bud brings osteoblasts + osteoclasts
  • Osteoblasts make spongy trabeculae; osteoclasts form medullary cavity
  • All long bones have 1° centres by week 12

Secondary centre (epiphysis, after birth)

  • Vessels enter epiphyses around birth
  • Spongy bone, no medullary cavity
  • Cartilage at ends stays as articular cartilage
  • Epiphyseal plate replaced by bone by ~20 years

Lecture 2

Limb development

Day 26–27Upper limb bud (lower: 1–2 days later), ventrolateral; upper opposite lower cervical segments, lower opposite lumbar + upper sacral
Week 5Hand and foot plates; mesenchymal bone models; motor axons enter
Week 6Hand digital rays (end); chondrification complete; synovial joints begin
Week 7Foot digital rays; 1° ossification begins; limb rotation
Week 8Digits separate (apoptosis); synovial joints complete
1st yearCarpal ossification begins (last)
  • Bud = mesenchyme from somatic lateral plate mesoderm covered by ectoderm. Bone and cartilage stay in the bud; muscles and nerves grow in; vessels form locally then join the aorta.
  • Apical ectodermal ridge (AER): thickened distal ectoderm secreting fibroblast growth factors (FGFs); keeps the progress zone proliferating; proximal mesenchyme differentiates into vessels and cartilage (proximodistal growth).
  • Zone of polarizing activity (ZPA): posterior mesenchyme next to AER; retinoic acid → sonic hedgehog (SHH); sets anteroposterior axis.
  • Hox genes: craniocaudal location. TBX5 forelimb, TBX4 hindlimb.
  • Digit separation: programmed cell death of loose mesenchyme between digital rays.

Clarifications

Confusing points fixed

  • Secondary ossification centres are in the epiphysis (not the metaphysis). Primary = diaphysis.
  • Two meanings of dermatome: somite part (makes dermis) vs clinical skin area of one spinal nerve root. Not the same.
  • Vertebral body halves: “dense of one + loose of the caudal segment” means the cranial (loose) half of the sclerotome below.
  • Limb tissue sources differ: bone, cartilage, tendons from somatic lateral plate mesoderm; muscle from somite myotome; Schwann cells from neural crest.
  • Slide typos: “Staps” = stapes; scaphocephaly is long and narrow; “periotic” = otic capsule.
  • Textbooks vary slightly on when limbs extend ventrally (late week 6 to early week 7); follow your slides: early week 7.
Summary

High-yield summary

Must-know numbers

  • Somites: end week 3
  • Limb buds: day 26–27 (week 4)
  • Plates: week 5; rays: wk 6 hand, wk 7 foot
  • 1° ossification: week 7; all by week 12
  • Digits free: week 8
  • Carpals: after birth

Origins

  • Nucleus pulposus: notochord
  • Face: neural crest
  • Parietal, occipital: paraxial
  • Laryngeal: lateral plate
  • Limb muscle: myotome
  • Schwann cells: neural crest

Mnemonics

  • TBX5 = 5 fingers (arm); TBX4 = lower limb
  • Upper limb rotates Laterally; lower rotates Medially
  • Epaxial = Erector (intrinsic back) muscles, dorsal ramus; hypaxial = the rest
